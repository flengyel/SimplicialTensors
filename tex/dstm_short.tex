\begin{abstract}
We compute the homology of simplicial modules generated by hypermatrices
over a field. Faces delete the same index along every
axis, and degeneracies duplicate it. The Python package
\emph{SimplicialTensors} implements these operations. Projecting finitely
many subtensors to normalized chains reduces homology to
linear algebra. A $2\times2$ matrix generates a module with
one-dimensional first homology, and three-axis tensors
realize the incidence complex of any finite graph. Over an infinite
field, generic kernel families have rational image closures in products
of Grassmannians. The full module associated to a shape is contractible.
With $k$ axes, its horn fillers are unique precisely above degree $k$.
On rooted trees the omitted-face map splits onto its image, which can
be a proper submodule of the boundary kernel. Every horn on a tree with
at least $k+2$ vertices fills uniquely. For $k\geq2$, a tree with $k+1$
vertices has an inner horn with $k!$ independent filling obstructions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Normalized chains of the simplicial module generated by a hypermatrix
can be computed by finite linear algebra. For a matrix, the face $d_i$
deletes row $i$ and column $i$, and the degeneracy $s_i$ duplicates both.
For a hypermatrix with more axes, delete or duplicate index $i$ along every axis.
In degree $n$, these operations act on the initial block of indices
$0,\ldots,n$ on every axis and carry along any indices after that
block. The alternating sum of the faces gives the chain differential.

Starting with a hypermatrix in degree $n$ over a field, take the linear spans of its
images under faces and degeneracies. To compute the normalized
chains in degree $p$, retain each choice of $p+1$ indices from the
initial block, using the same choice on every axis, and project the
resulting hypermatrices to normalized chains. They span the degree-$p$
chain group. There are no normalized chains above degree $n$, so
the homology follows from finite linear algebra
(Section~\ref{sec:generated}).

The \href{https://github.com/flengyel/SimplicialTensors}{\emph{SimplicialTensors}
repository} supplies the simplicial operations and examples. The module
generated by the $2\times2$ matrix with a single $1$ in position
$(0,1)$ has one-dimensional first homology
(Example~\ref{ex:matrix-cycle}). Encoding the vertices and edges
of a graph in matrix slices of hypermatrices with three axes gives the
graph's incidence complex (Proposition~\ref{prop:graph}). For the
Petersen graph the computation gives first Betti number six.

A coordinate basis gives the full module's horn kernels and disk
decomposition (Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis} and Proposition~\ref{prop:disks}).
Section~\ref{sec:tree-horns} computes omitted-face lifts and a sharp
horn-filling bound on rooted trees. Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-families}
parameterizes generic normalized kernels in Grassmannians and proves
rationality of their image closure.

\section{The construction}
Throughout, $A$ is a nonzero commutative ring with identity, $k\geq1$, and
$[p]=\{0,\ldots,p\}$. Let $\Delta$ denote the category of finite nonempty
ordinals and nondecreasing morphisms.

Fix a shape
$\vec s=(n_1,\ldots,n_k)\in\mathbb Z_{>0}^k$ and put
\[
 n=\min_a n_a-1,\qquad c_a=n_a-1-n,\qquad
 I_p=\prod_{a=1}^k[p+c_a].
\]
The integers $c_a=n_a-\min_b n_b$ are the \emph{shape offsets}.
The offsets are nonnegative and at least one is zero. They determine the
simplicial module, and $n$ is the degree in which the original shape
occurs. We allow arbitrary nonnegative tail lengths
$\mathbf c=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$ and use $X(\mathbf c;A)$ for the resulting
module. A common rank shift $r\geq0$ replaces the offsets of a shape by
$(c_a+r)_a$.

For $c\geq0$, put $J_c([p])=[p+c]$. For $\alpha:[p]\to[q]$, define
\[
 J_c(\alpha)(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 \alpha(r),&0\leq r\leq p,\\
 q+t,&r=p+t,\quad1\leq t\leq c.
 \end{cases}
\]
This defines a covariant functor $J_c:\Delta\to\mathbf{Set}$, since
composition acts separately on the initial ordinal and the final $c$
elements. Consequently $I=\prod_a J_{c_a}$ is cosimplicial and
\[
 X_p=X_p(\mathbf c;A)=A^{I_p},\qquad
 X(\alpha)(T)=T\circ I(\alpha)
\]
defines a functor $X:\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_A$.
This is the \emph{diagonal simplicial tensor module} with tail lengths
$\mathbf c$. For the offsets of a shape $\vec s$, write $X(\vec s;A)$
for this same module. Write $E_m$ for the indicator function of
$m\in I_p$.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the index coface
$\Delta_i:I_{p-1}\to I_p$ inserts a gap at $i$ in
every coordinate; its image consists of the tuples avoiding $i$.
For $p\geq0$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the codegeneracy
$\Sigma_i:I_{p+1}\to I_p$ identifies $i$ and $i+1$ in every coordinate. Thus $d_iT=T\circ\Delta_i$ and
$s_iT=T\circ\Sigma_i$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:face}
 d_iE_m=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&i\in\im(m),\\
 E_{\Delta_i^{-1}m},&i\notin\im(m),
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \im(m)=\{m_1,\ldots,m_k\}.
\end{equation}
The nonzero images in this formula are distinct basis elements.
There are no outgoing face maps in degree zero. The associated chain
complex has $X_{-1}=0$ and differential
\[
 \partial_p=\sum_{i=0}^p(-1)^id_i\quad(p\geq1),\qquad \partial_0=0.
\]
For a simplicial module $Y$, we write $H_p(Y)$ for the homology
of this unaugmented complex. For instance, the faces of the vector
$(3,5)$ are $(5)$ and $(3)$, so its boundary is $(2)$.

The module $X$ is the diagonal of the $k$-fold simplicial module with
term $A^{\prod_a[p_a+c_a]}$ in multidegree $(p_1,\ldots,p_k)$.
Compare Quillen's diagonal of a double semisimplicial group
\cite{Quillen1966}. The finite coordinate bases also identify
$X(\mathbf c;A)\otimes_A B$ with $X(\mathbf c;B)$ for any ring
homomorphism $A\to B$.

For $T\in X_n$, its generated simplicial submodule has degree-$p$ part
\[
 \langle T\rangle_p=
 \operatorname{span}_A\{X(\alpha)(T):\alpha:[p]\to[n]\}.
\]
This is closed under faces and degeneracies by functoriality.

\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-cycle}
Over a field $K$, take shape $(2,2)$, write $E_{ij}=E_{(i,j)}$ and let
\[
 T=E_{01}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Y=\langle T\rangle\subseteq X.
\]
Both faces vanish, so $Y_0=0$, $Y_1=KT$, and
\[
 Y_2=\operatorname{span}_K\{s_0T,s_1T\},\qquad
 s_0T=E_{02}+E_{12},\quad s_1T=E_{01}+E_{02}.
\]
Both degeneracies have zero boundary by the simplicial identities.
Thus $H_1(Y)\cong K$. In the ambient complex, $\partial_2E_{12}=T$.
The tensor $E_{12}$ lies outside $Y_2$ because the two displayed
generators have coordinates satisfying $x_{02}=x_{12}+x_{01}$.
\end{example}

The accompanying script \path{dstm_examples.py} reproduces
Examples~\ref{ex:matrix-cycle} and~\ref{ex:matrix-horn} using
\emph{SimplicialTensors} \cite{SimplicialTensors}. It computes the
first homology dimension over $\mathbb Q$ and the two free horn
coordinates.

\section{Horn kernels and normalization}
For any simplicial $A$-module $Y$, put
\[
 N_p(Y)=\bigcap_{i=1}^p\ker d_i,\qquad
 D_p(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\im s_i.
\]
Here $N_0(Y)=Y_0$, $D_0(Y)=0$, and the normalized differential is
$d_0$ in positive degrees and zero in degree zero; this is the convention
in \cite[Exercise 8.3.4]{Weibel}. The normalization theorem gives
\mbox{$Y_p=N_p(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$} \cite[Corollary 1.12]{Dold1958}.
The Dold--Kan theorem gives an equivalence with nonnegative chain
complexes \cite[Theorem 8.4.1]{Weibel}.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$, let $R_{p,j}(Y)=
\bigcap_{0\leq i\leq p,\ i\ne j}\ker d_i$.
The module $\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$ of compatible horns consists of tuples
$(y_i)_{i\ne j}$ with $y_i\in Y_{p-1}$ satisfying $d_i y_\ell=d_{\ell-1}y_i$ for
$i<\ell$, $i,\ell\ne j$; for $p=1$ there are no compatibility
conditions. The horn map is
$\Phi_j:Y_p\to\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$, $y\mapsto(d_i y)_{i\ne j}$.
Every simplicial module is Kan, so $\Phi_j$ is surjective
\cite[Lemma 8.2.8]{Weibel}. Its fibers are torsors for $R_{p,j}(Y)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hornsplit}
For every simplicial $A$-module $Y$, $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$,
\[
 Y_p=R_{p,j}(Y)\oplus D_p(Y).
\]
The restriction $\Phi_j|_{D_p(Y)}$ is an isomorphism. Thus every horn
has a unique filler in the submodule $D_p(Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\leq j<p$, the simplicial identities give inverse maps
\[
 F_j=1-s_jd_j:R_{p,j}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j+1}(Y),\qquad
 G_j=1-s_jd_{j+1}:R_{p,j+1}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j}(Y).
\]
The faces of $s_jd_jx$ for $x\in R_{p,j}(Y)$ vanish away
from $j,j+1$. For $i<j$ use $d_id_j=d_{j-1}d_i$, and for
$i>j+1$ use $d_{i-1}d_j=d_jd_i$ after moving $d_i$ past $s_j$.
The identities $d_js_j=d_{j+1}s_j=1$ give $d_jF_jx=0$ and
$d_{j+1}F_jx=-d_jx$, so $G_jF_jx=x$. For $y\in R_{p,j+1}(Y)$ the
same face identities applied to $s_jd_{j+1}y$ give
$d_{j+1}G_jy=0$ and $d_jG_jy=-d_{j+1}y$, so $F_jG_jy=y$.
Both maps induce the identity modulo $D_p(Y)$. Since
$R_{p,0}(Y)=N_p(Y)$ maps isomorphically onto $Y_p/D_p(Y)$,
the same holds for every $R_{p,j}(Y)$. This proves the direct sum.
The final assertion follows from surjectivity of $\Phi_j$ and
$\ker\Phi_j=R_{p,j}(Y)$.
\end{proof}

For simplicial objects in finite-dimensional real vector spaces the horn-space
isomorphism is also proved in \cite[Lemma 2.10]{RonchiZhu};
Lemma~\ref{lem:hornsplit} gives an argument over arbitrary $A$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hornbasis}
Let $X=X(\mathbf c;A)$, $p\geq1$, and $0\leq j\leq p$. Put
$F_j=[p]\setminus\{j\}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hornbasis}
 R_{p,j}(X)=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\F_j\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m,
 \qquad
 \rank R_{p,j}(X)=
 \sum_{t=0}^p(-1)^t\binom pt\prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t).
\end{equation}
In particular, $R_{p,j}(X)\ne0$ if and only if $p\leq k$.
Thus horn fillers are nonunique for $1\leq p\leq k$ and unique
for $p>k$, independently of $j$ and of the offsets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:face}, $d_iT=0$ exactly when $T$ is supported on
tuples containing $i$. Intersecting these support conditions gives
\eqref{eq:hornbasis}. Inclusion--exclusion counts tuples containing
all $p$ elements of $F_j$: forbidding a specified $t$ of these
elements leaves $p+c_a+1-t$ choices in coordinate $a$.
A tuple of length $k$ cannot contain $p>k$ distinct values. If
$p\leq k$, assign the $p$ required values to distinct coordinates
and fill the remaining coordinates arbitrarily in $[p]$.
This gives a basis element, nonzero because $A\ne0$.
\end{proof}


\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-horn}
For shape $(3,3)$, consider the $2$-horn with missing face $0$ and
prescribed faces
\[
 d_1T=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\7&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 d_2T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\4&5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their top left entries agree, so the faces are compatible. Every filler is of the form
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\4&5&u\\7&v&9\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad u,v\in A.
\]
Thus the difference of two fillers lies in
$R_{2,0}(X)=AE_{(1,2)}\oplus AE_{(2,1)}$, whose basis consists
of the two index pairs containing both $1$ and $2$.
\end{example}

An algebraic $r$-hypergroupoid is a simplicial module with unique
horn fillers in dimensions greater than $r$ \cite{Duskin1979,Glenn1982}.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis}, $X$ is such an object exactly when $r\geq k$.
For the original degree $n=\min_a n_a-1$, the $n$-hypergroupoid
condition is $k\leq n$, with nonunique $n$-horn fillers when $k=n\geq1$.

\section{The normalized complex}
Write $B=A^{\prod_a C_a}$, where $C_a$ is the set of $c_a$ tail
labels on axis $a$. At degree $p$ the tail labels are
$p+1,\ldots,p+c_a$. Projection onto tuples of tail labels defines a
simplicial morphism $X\to\underline B$, with section given by extension
by zero. Write $Z_0=\ker(X_0\to B)$ and
$Z_p=\ker(d_0:N_p(X)\to N_{p-1}(X))$ for $p\geq1$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
 Z_p=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\{}[p]\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m
 \qquad(p\geq0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:disks}
Let $X=X(\mathbf c;A)$. For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$,
the extension by zero $\iota_j:Z_{p-1}\to R_{p,j}(X)$ along
$\Delta_j$ gives
$R_{p,j}(X)=Z_p\oplus\iota_j Z_{p-1}$ and $d_j\iota_j=1$.
If $D^r(M)$ denotes the identity complex $M\xrightarrow{1}M$
in degrees $r,r-1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disks}
 N_\bullet(X)\cong B[0]\oplus\bigoplus_{p=0}^{k-1}D^{p+1}(Z_p).
\end{equation}
In particular $H_0(X)\cong A^{\prod_a c_a}$ and $H_p(X)=0$ for $p>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Partition the basis of $R_{p,j}(X)$ according as the tuple contains
$j$ or avoids it. The tuples containing $j$ give the basis of $Z_p$.
Deleting the gap at $j$ sends the other tuples bijectively to the
basis of $Z_{p-1}$, and $d_j$ induces this bijection by
\eqref{eq:face}. For $j=0$ this is the normalized differential.
In degree zero $X_0=B\oplus Z_0$. Finally $Z_p=0$ for $p\geq k$,
since a tuple of length $k$ cannot contain $p+1$ distinct indices.
\end{proof}

The contraction on the disk summands sends $E_m\in Z_p$ to
$E_{m+\mathbf1}\in N_{p+1}(X)$ and vanishes on the other summands.
The coordinate bases show that normalized chains, cycles and horn
kernels commute with arbitrary base change. In degree zero the cycles
are $B\oplus Z_0$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ranks}
Let $b_p=\rank Z_p$ and $b_{-1}=0$. Then
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 b_p=\sum_{t=0}^{p+1}(-1)^t\binom{p+1}{t}
          \prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t),\\
 \rank N_p(X)=b_p+b_{p-1}+\mathbf 1_{p=0}\prod_a c_a.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For $c_a=0$ on all axes, $b_p=(p+1)!\SII{k}{p+1}$, where
$\SII{k}{r}$ is a Stirling number of the second kind.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply inclusion--exclusion to \eqref{eq:cycles} and use
\eqref{eq:disks}. With all tails empty, tuples covering $[p]$
are surjections from a $k$-element set onto $[p]$
\cite[\S1.4]{StanleyEC1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For square matrices with empty tails,
$N_\bullet(X)\cong D^1(A)\oplus D^2(A^2)$.
The normalized ranks are $1,3,2$ in degrees $0,1,2$. The
basis elements $E_{(1,2)}$ and $E_{(2,1)}$ in degree $2$ map
to $E_{(0,1)}$ and $E_{(1,0)}$ under $d_0$.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:shape}
Over a fixed nonzero commutative ring, two modules $X(\mathbf c;A)$
are isomorphic precisely when their multisets of tail lengths agree.
For modules specified by shapes, this means permuting the entries
and adding the same integer to each entry.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An isomorphism equates the ranks in every degree. Hence the
polynomials $\prod_a(x+c_a+1)$ agree on all nonnegative integers,
so their roots agree with multiplicities. A permutation matching
the tails induces a simplicial isomorphism on coordinates.
\end{proof}

\section{Split contractions and rank shifts}
For $A$-modules $E,W$, put $V_A(E,W)_p=E^{p+1}\oplus W$.
Faces delete and degeneracies repeat $E$-coordinates, fixing $W$.
This is the \v{C}ech nerve of $E\oplus W\to W$ \cite[Tag 019P]{StacksCech}.
On $V=\bigotimes_a V_A(E_a,W_a)$ the split projections give simplicial
morphisms $\epsilon:V\to\underline{B}$ and $j:\underline{B}\to V$,
where $B=\bigotimes_a W_a$. Taking $E_a=A$ and $W_a=A^{c_a}$
recovers $X(\mathbf c;A)$.

Let $H_p:V_p\to V_{p+1}$ be the tensor product of the maps
$(e_0,\ldots,e_p,w)\mapsto(0,e_0,\ldots,e_p,w)$. In degree $-1$
take $H_{-1}=j_0:B\to V_0$. Then $d_0H_p=1$ and
$d_iH_p=H_{p-1}d_{i-1}$ for $1\leq i\leq p+1$, using
$d_0=\epsilon_0$ on $V_0$ in the latter identity. Summing with signs gives
$\partial_{p+1}H_p+H_{p-1}\partial_p=1$ for $p\geq1$ and
$\partial_1H_0=1-j_0\epsilon_0$. Hence the unaugmented complex
deformation retracts onto $B[0]$. Projecting $H$ to the normalized
summand gives the same retraction on $N(V)$. For the offsets of a shape,
some $c_a=0$, so $B=0$ and the full complex is contractible.

For coordinate tensors, $H_p(E_m)=E_{m+\mathbf1}$. Both this contraction
and the normalized contraction commute with axis permutations preserving
the tails, so they restrict to the symmetric and sign eigenspaces.
For square complex matrices they commute with conjugate transpose on
the underlying real complexes. If some tail is empty, the filtration
spanned by $E_m$ with $\min_a m_a\geq p+t$, indexed by $t\in\mathbb Z$,
is decreasing and finite in each degree. A surviving face lowers the minimum by at most one,
and $H_p$ raises it by one. The induced contraction on the associated
graded complex gives zero homology.

For the two-variable form of the rank shift, let $\Delta_+$ be
$\Delta$ with the empty ordinal $[-1]$ adjoined. For $p,q\geq-1$
use the index set
\[
 I_{p,q}=\prod_{a=1}^k\bigl(([p]\oplus[c_a-1])\oplus[q]\bigr),
\]
where $\oplus$ denotes ordinal sum. On a pair of morphisms
$(\alpha,\beta):([p],[q])\to([p'],[q'])$, each factor uses
$\alpha\oplus 1_{[c_a-1]}\oplus\beta$. Pullback gives a functor
$(\Delta_+\times\Delta_+)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_A$.
Its slice at $q$ is the horizontally augmented module with
tail lengths $c_a+q+1$. Thus $r=q+1$, and $q=-1$ gives $r=0$. Moving the fixed block
after $[q]$ identifies this bifunctor with the pullback of the augmented
$X$ along ordinal sum. The degree-zero faces into degree $-1$ restrict
to the surviving blocks.
Horizontal and vertical maps commute because they act on separate
blocks. For the alternating face boundaries, the total differential
on bidegree $(p,q)$ is $\partial_h+(-1)^p\partial_v$.
The horizontal contraction prepends zero on the first block and
commutes with all vertical maps. It contracts the total complex
when the horizontal augmentation is included.
