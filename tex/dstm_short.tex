\begin{abstract}
We compute the homology of the simplicial submodule generated by a hypermatrix $T$ over a field.
The shape $(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ of $T$ determines its simplicial dimension $n=\min_a n_a-1$ and its excesses $\mathbf c=(n_a-n-1)_a$.
In degree $p$ the diagonal simplicial tensor module $X^{\mathbf c}$ has $p+1+c_a$ indices on axis $a$.
The full chain complex is contractible for these excesses. For $q\geq1$, a submodule with nonzero $H_q$ requires at least $q+1$ axes. The degree-$q$ tensor whose sole nonzero entry is $1$ at $(0,1,\ldots,q)$ attains this bound.
Restrict $T$ to $p+1$ common indices in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ together with the $c_a$ indices above $n$ on each axis $a$.
The projections of these restrictions span the degree-$p$ normalized chains of the submodule.
The package \emph{SimplicialTensors} computes its Betti numbers from the ranks of these columns and their boundaries over $\mathbb Q$ and finite prime fields.
Three-axis tensors realize the incidence complex of a finite graph and hence its ordinary homology.
For a simple graph on $m\geq2$ vertices, insert an isolated vertex between consecutive vertices of a given order.
The mod-$2$ boundary of the resulting adjacency matrix has full rank precisely when the original order traces a Hamiltonian path.
Counting the orders of full rank gives $22{,}394{,}880$ oriented Hamiltonian paths for the $4\times4$ rook graph and $22{,}230{,}528$ for the cospectral Shrikhande graph.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
We compute the homology of the simplicial submodule generated by a hypermatrix
$T$ over a field $K$. Its shape $(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ determines its simplicial
dimension $n=\min_a n_a-1$ and its excesses $\mathbf c=(n_a-n-1)_{a=1}^k$.
In degree $p$ the diagonal simplicial tensor module $X^{\mathbf c}(K)$ has
$p+1+c_a$ indices on axis $a$. The face $d_i$ deletes index $i$ on every axis
and the degeneracy $s_i$ repeats it. Since $\min_a c_a=0$, inserting a zero slice on
each axis contracts the full chain complex (Section~\ref{sec:contractions}).
The contraction need not preserve the generated submodule. For the $2\times2$ matrix
unit $E_{01}$, this submodule has first homology $K$ (Example~\ref{ex:matrix-cycle}).

To compute the normalized chains in degree $p$, retain on each axis $a$ the same
$p+1$ indices in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ together with $n+1,\ldots,n+c_a$.
Project these restrictions to normalized chains.
Write $r_p$ for the rank of the resulting $\binom{n+1}{p+1}$ columns and
$\delta_p$ for the rank of their images under the differential, with
$\delta_0=0$. The $p$th Betti number is $r_p-\delta_p-\delta_{p+1}$.
The normalized chains vanish for $p>\min(n,k)$ (Section~\ref{sec:generated}).
The Python package \emph{SimplicialTensors} implements this calculation
over $\mathbb Q$ and prime fields \cite{SimplicialTensors}.
The repository at \url{https://github.com/flengyel/SimplicialTensors} includes a
\href{https://github.com/flengyel/SimplicialTensors/blob/0cd9a0a6f7b080989f535df6c2f183266eb05976/tex/README.md}{guide to the programs and commands for the examples and tables}.

For $q\geq1$, the basis tensor $E_{(0,1,\ldots,q)}$ on $q+1$ axes generates
the reduced free simplicial $K$-module on the sphere $\Delta^q/\partial\Delta^q$.
A normalized $q$-cycle is supported on index tuples containing all of $0,\ldots,q$.
Each tuple has one entry per axis, so on fewer than $q+1$ axes every submodule has
zero $q$th homology (Example~\ref{ex:sparse-higher-cycle}).
An integral $3\times3$ matrix
generates a submodule with first Betti number one over $\mathbb Q$ and zero
over $\mathbb F_3$ (Example~\ref{ex:parameter-jump}).

Three-axis tensors encode the vertices and oriented edges of a finite
graph in degrees zero and one. The normalized chains of the submodule they
generate form the graph's incidence complex, with boundary sending an edge
to its target minus its source. Its homology is therefore ordinary graph
homology (Proposition~\ref{prop:graph}). For the Petersen graph the incidence
map has rank nine and the first Betti number is six.

We also compute mod-$2$ boundary ranks of ordered adjacency matrices.
For the original adjacency matrices of the $4\times4$ rook and Shrikhande
graphs, Table~\ref{tab:affine-ranks} counts the $1{,}536$ affine orders of each
Cayley presentation on $(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2$.
To count Hamiltonian paths in a simple graph on $m\geq2$ vertices, insert
one isolated vertex between successive vertices of each vertex order $\pi$
(Figure~\ref{fig:interleaved-path}). The mod-$2$ boundary of the adjacency
matrix in this order has rank $2m-2$ precisely when $\pi$ traces a
Hamiltonian path (Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-graph-ranks}).
Table~\ref{tab:interleaved-ranks} gives the boundary ranks
after insertion over all orders of the original vertices for the Petersen,
rook and Shrikhande graphs.
Rank $30$ occurs in $22{,}394{,}880$ orders of the
$4\times4$ rook graph and $22{,}230{,}528$ orders of the cospectral
Shrikhande graph. The rook count agrees with the number of oriented
Hamiltonian paths in OEIS A096970 \cite{OEISRook}. The maximum rank is
$2(m-p)$, where $p$ is the minimum number of vertex-disjoint paths covering
the graph.

Fix the generator degree $n$ and the ambient module. Let $C$ be the normalized
chains over $K$ of the standard $n$-simplex and $\mathcal D$ the ambient normalized
chains. Each generator $T$ induces a chain map $g_T:C\to\mathcal D$. Two such
maps have equal kernels exactly when an isomorphism of the generated modules
sends one generator to the other. Every chain isomorphism between quotients
of $C$ lifts to a chain automorphism of $C$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift}).
Thus, after forgetting the generators, the isomorphism classes correspond to
the orbits of the kernels under chain automorphisms of $C$.

A subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ is the kernel of some $g_T$ exactly when the
dimensions of cycles and boundaries of $C/L$ do not exceed the respective
dimensions for $\mathcal D$ in any degree (Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel-realizability}).
On the locus where every component of $g_T$ has maximal rank, the kernels define a family in
a product of Grassmannians. Over an infinite field the reduced Zariski closure
of this family is rational. For $3\times3$ matrices the image is the dual
projective plane minus the point $[1:1:1]$, and its $K$-points form one orbit
(Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-families}).

A linear tree with $p$ vertices represents simplicial degree $p$.
For $k$ tensor axes every simplicial horn in degree $p\geq1$ fills, and the filler is unique exactly when $p\geq k+1$.
Thus $X^{\mathbf c}$ is an $n$-hypergroupoid exactly when $n\geq k$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis}).
On arbitrary rooted trees every horn fills uniquely from $k+2$ vertices onward.
When $k\geq2$, a branching tree with $k+1$ vertices already admits a compatible inner horn with no filler.
For $k=1$ every inner horn fills uniquely (Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-cutoff}).

\section{The construction}
Let $A$ be a nonzero commutative ring with identity and $k\geq1$. The objects of
$\Delta$ are the ordinals $[p]=\{0,\ldots,p\}$ for $p\geq0$, and its morphisms are the order-preserving maps.

A hypermatrix of \emph{shape} $(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ has \emph{simplicial dimension}
$n=\min_a n_a-1$ and excesses $\mathbf c=(n_a-n-1)_{a=1}^k$. Thus $\min_a c_a=0$.
For any tuple $\mathbf c$ of nonnegative integers, put $I_q=\prod_a[q+c_a]$.
In degree $q$, the \emph{excess on axis $a$} is the number $c_a$ of indices strictly
greater than $q$. These are $q+1,\ldots,q+c_a$. The axis length is $q+1+c_a$,
which recovers $n_a$ in degree $n$ for the given shape.

With $[-1]$ the empty ordinal, put $J_c([p])=[p]\oplus[c-1]=[p+c]$
for $c\geq0$, where $\oplus$ is the ordinal sum \cite[Ch.~VII, \S5, p.~175]{MacLaneCategories}.
For $\alpha:[p]\to[q]$,
$J_c(\alpha)=\alpha\oplus 1_{[c-1]}$ sends $i\in[p]$
to $\alpha(i)$ and $p+t$ to $q+t$ for $1\leq t\leq c$.
The product $I=\prod_a J_{c_a}:\Delta\to\mathbf{Set}$ is a cosimplicial set.
The \emph{diagonal simplicial tensor module} $X=X^{\mathbf c}(A)$ has
$X_p=A^{I_p}$, the module of hypermatrices $T:I_p\to A$.
For $\alpha:[p]\to[q]$, the simplicial operator $X(\alpha):X_q\to X_p$ is precomposition
with $I(\alpha):I_p\to I_q$, so $X(\alpha)(T)=T\circ I(\alpha)$.
In multidegree $(p_1,\ldots,p_k)$ the coordinate bases give an isomorphism of $A$-modules
\[
 A^{[p_1+c_1]}\otimes_A\cdots\otimes_A A^{[p_k+c_k]}
 \ \cong\ A^{\prod_a[p_a+c_a]},\qquad
 e_{m_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{m_k}\longmapsto E_m.
\]
Here $e_j$ and $E_m$ are the characteristic functions of $\{j\}$ and
$\{m\}$, where $m=(m_1,\ldots,m_k)$. Precomposition in each coordinate acts on the
corresponding factor. Restricting this $k$-fold simplicial module along the diagonal $\alpha\mapsto(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)$
gives $X$. For $k=2$, Quillen used this diagonal for bisimplicial groups \cite{Quillen1966}.

In degree $q$ the coordinate basis gives $\rank_A X_q=\prod_a(q+1+c_a)$.
For shape $(3,5)$ we have $n=2$ and $\mathbf c=(0,2)$, so $\rank_A X_q=(q+1)(q+3)$
(\program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L25}{shape_example}).

For general $\mathbf c$, the shape in degree $p$ has simplicial dimension
$p+\min_a c_a$. A $3\times5$ matrix placed in degree one has $\mathbf c=(1,3)$.
Positive excesses on every axis occur in Section~\ref{sec:contractions}.
The coordinate bases identify $X^{\mathbf c}(A)\otimes_A B$ with
$X^{\mathbf c}(B)$ for any ring homomorphism $A\to B$.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the index coface
$\Delta_i:I_{p-1}\to I_p$ skips $i$ in every coordinate.
For $p\geq0$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the codegeneracy
$\Sigma_i:I_{p+1}\to I_p$ identifies $i$ and $i+1$ in every coordinate. Thus $d_iT=T\circ\Delta_i$ and
$s_iT=T\circ\Sigma_i$. For $m\in I_p$, write $\im(m)=\{m_1,\ldots,m_k\}$.
If $i\notin\im(m)$, then $m$ has a unique preimage $\Delta_i^{-1}m$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:face}
 d_iE_m=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&i\in\im(m),\\
 E_{\Delta_i^{-1}m},&i\notin\im(m).
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For any simplicial module $Y$, write $H_p(Y)$ for the homology of its
unaugmented chain complex, with differential
$\partial_p=\sum_{i=0}^p(-1)^i d_i$ for $p\geq1$ and $\partial_0=0$.
For the vector $v=(3,5)$ over $\mathbb Z$, $\partial_1 v=(5)-(3)=(2)$
(\program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L42}{vector_example}).

For $T\in X_n$, the simplicial submodule $\langle T\rangle$ generated by
$T$ has degree-$p$ term spanned by $X(\alpha)(T)$ for $\alpha:[p]\to[n]$.

\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-cycle}
Over a field $K$, take the matrix unit $T=E_{01}$ in degree one of $X^{(0,0)}(K)$
and let $Y=\langle T\rangle$. Here $E_{ij}=E_{(i,j)}$. Both faces of $T$ vanish,
so $X(\alpha)(T)=0$ for every constant map $\alpha:[p]\to[1]$. Thus $Y_0=0$ and $Y_1=KT$.
The two surjections $[2]\to[1]$ give a spanning set $s_0T,s_1T$ for $Y_2$, with
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 s_0T=E_{02}+E_{12},\quad s_1T=E_{01}+E_{02}.
\]
The simplicial identities give $\partial_2s_0T=s_0d_1T=0$ and $\partial_2s_1T=s_0d_0T=0$.
Thus $H_1(Y)\cong K$. In the ambient complex, $\partial_2E_{12}=T$.
Every matrix in $Y_2$ satisfies $x_{02}=x_{12}+x_{01}$, so $E_{12}\notin Y_2$.
See \program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L54}{generated_homology_example} for the calculation over $\mathbb Q$.
\end{example}

\section{Horn kernels and normalization}
For a simplicial $A$-module $Y$, the normalized and degenerate chains in
degree $p$ are, respectively,
\[
 N_p(Y)=\bigcap_{i=1}^p\ker d_i,\qquad
 D_p(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\im s_i.
\]
Here $N_0(Y)=Y_0$ and $D_0(Y)=0$. The differential on $N(Y)$ is
$d_0$ in positive degrees and zero in degree zero
\cite[Exercise 8.3.4]{Weibel}. Reversing the face and degeneracy indices
in Dold's splitting \cite[Corollary 1.12]{Dold1958} gives
$Y_p=N_p(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$. The inclusion of normalized chains
induces $H_p(N(Y))\cong H_p(Y)$ \cite[Theorem 8.3.8]{Weibel}.
Normalization gives the Dold--Kan equivalence with nonnegative chain
complexes \cite[Theorem 8.4.1]{Weibel}.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$, let $R_{p,j}(Y)=
\bigcap_{0\leq i\leq p,\ i\ne j}\ker d_i$.
The module $\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$ of compatible horns consists of tuples
$(y_i)_{i\ne j}$ with $y_i\in Y_{p-1}$ satisfying $d_i y_\ell=d_{\ell-1}y_i$
for $i<\ell$, $i,\ell\ne j$. This condition is vacuous for $p=1$.
The horn map is
$\Phi_j:Y_p\to\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$, $y\mapsto(d_i y)_{i\ne j}$.
Every simplicial module is Kan, so $\Phi_j$ is surjective
\cite[Lemma 8.2.8]{Weibel}. If $x$ fills a horn, its fillers form the
coset $x+R_{p,j}(Y)$.

Ashley's theorem on horn fillers in $D_p(Y)$ applies to the underlying
simplicial abelian group \cite[Ch.~3, Theorem~1.5]{Ashley1978}.
For simplicial vector spaces see Ronchi and Zhu \cite[Lemma~2.10]{RonchiZhu}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hornsplit}
Let $Y$ be a simplicial $A$-module, $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$.
Then $Y_p=R_{p,j}(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$, and the horn map restricts to an
isomorphism $D_p(Y)\to\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\leq j<p$, the simplicial identities give inverse maps
\[
 F_j=1-s_jd_j:R_{p,j}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j+1}(Y),\qquad
 G_j=1-s_jd_{j+1}:R_{p,j+1}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j}(Y).
\]
The faces of $s_jd_jx$ for $x\in R_{p,j}(Y)$ vanish away
from $j,j+1$. For $i<j$ use $d_id_j=d_{j-1}d_i$, and for
$i>j+1$ use $d_{i-1}d_j=d_jd_i$ after moving $d_i$ past $s_j$.
The identities $d_js_j=d_{j+1}s_j=1$ give $d_jF_jx=0$ and
$d_{j+1}F_jx=-d_jx$, so $G_jF_jx=x$. For $y\in R_{p,j+1}(Y)$ the
same face identities applied to $s_jd_{j+1}y$ give
$d_{j+1}G_jy=0$ and $d_jG_jy=-d_{j+1}y$, so $F_jG_jy=y$.
Both maps induce the identity modulo $D_p(Y)$. Since
$R_{p,0}(Y)=N_p(Y)$ maps isomorphically onto $Y_p/D_p(Y)$,
the same holds for every $R_{p,j}(Y)$. Since $\Phi_j$ is surjective with
kernel $R_{p,j}(Y)$, its restriction to $D_p(Y)$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hornbasis}
Let $X=X^{\mathbf c}(A)$, $p\geq1$, and $0\leq j\leq p$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hornbasis}
 R_{p,j}(X)=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\{}[p]\setminus\{j\}\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m,
 \qquad
 \rank R_{p,j}(X)=
 \sum_{t=0}^p(-1)^t\binom pt\prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t).
\end{equation}
Consequently, every horn has more than one filler for $p\leq k$ and a
unique filler for $p>k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:face}, $d_iT=0$ exactly when $T$ is supported on
tuples containing $i$. Intersecting these support conditions gives
the basis in \eqref{eq:hornbasis}. To count its elements by
inclusion--exclusion, forbid $t$ of the $p$ required labels.
This leaves $p+c_a+1-t$ choices in coordinate $a$.
A tuple of length $k$ cannot contain $p>k$ distinct values. If
$p\leq k$, assign the $p$ required values to distinct coordinates
and fill the remaining coordinates arbitrarily in $[p]$.
This gives a basis element since $A\ne0$.
\end{proof}


\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-horn}
For shape $(3,3)$, take the $2$-horn with missing face $0$ and prescribed
faces $y_1,y_2$ below. They agree in their upper left entry, and their
fillers are the displayed matrices $T$ as $u,v$ range over $A$.
\[
 y_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\7&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 y_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\4&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\4&5&u\\7&v&9\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The unique filler in $D_2(X)$ is $s_1y_2+s_0(y_1-y_2)$, which gives
$u=6$ and $v=8$. The matrix units $E_{(1,2)}$ and $E_{(2,1)}$
form a basis of $R_{2,0}(X)$. The function \program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L101}{horn_example}
computes the filler and verifies this kernel basis.
\end{example}

For $r\geq0$, a simplicial $A$-module is an $r$-hypergroupoid if its
underlying Kan complex has unique horn fillers in dimensions greater
than $r$ \cite{Duskin1979,Glenn1982}. By Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis},
the least such $r$ for $X$ is $k$.
For a shape of simplicial dimension $n$, the $n$-hypergroupoid
condition is $k\leq n$.

\section{The normalized complex}\label{sec:normalized}
Put $C_a=\{1,\ldots,c_a\}$ and $B=A^{\prod_a C_a}$. Restriction to
the tuples $(p+t_1,\ldots,p+t_k)$ with $t_a\in C_a$ defines a simplicial
morphism $\epsilon:X\to\underline B$ to the constant module on $B$. Extension by zero gives a
section $\eta$. Let $Z_p$ denote the cycles of $N(X)$ augmented by
$\epsilon_0:X_0\to B$. In degree zero $Z_0=\ker\epsilon_0$ is spanned by
the $E_m$ with $0\in\im(m)$. In positive degrees cycles lie in the kernel of
every face. Hence \eqref{eq:face} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
 Z_p=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\{}[p]\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m
 \qquad(p\geq0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:disks}
Let $X=X^{\mathbf c}(A)$. For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$, the sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow Z_p\longrightarrow R_{p,j}(X)
 \xrightarrow{d_j} Z_{p-1}\longrightarrow0
\]
is split exact, with section $\iota_j$ of $d_j$ given by extension by
zero along $\Delta_j$.
Write $D^r(M)$ for the complex $M\xrightarrow{1}M$ in degrees $r,r-1$.
The unaugmented normalized complex decomposes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disks}
 N_\bullet(X)\cong B[0]\oplus\bigoplus_{p=0}^{k-1}D^{p+1}(Z_p).
\end{equation}
In particular $H_0(X)\cong A^{\prod_a c_a}$ and $H_p(X)=0$ for $p>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $E_m$ in the basis of $R_{p,j}(X)$ with $j\in\im(m)$ span $Z_p$.
Deleting the gap at $j$ sends the remaining units bijectively to the basis of
$Z_{p-1}$ by \eqref{eq:face}, with inverse $\iota_j$.
For $j=0$ this gives $N_p(X)=Z_p\oplus\iota_0 Z_{p-1}$.
The differential is zero on $Z_p$ and inverts $\iota_0$ on the second summand.
In degree zero $X_0=\eta_0(B)\oplus Z_0$, and $Z_p=0$ for $p\geq k$
since a $k$-tuple cannot contain all of $[p]$.
\end{proof}

If $E_m$ is a coordinate unit of $N_p(X)$, then $m$ contains $1,\ldots,p$.
If $m$ also contains $0$, set
$h_pE_m=E_{m+\mathbf1}$, where $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$. Then
$\partial E_m=0$ and $\partial h_pE_m=E_m$.
If $m$ omits $0$, set $h_pE_m=0$. For $p>0$ we have
$\partial E_m=E_{m-\mathbf1}$, and $m-\mathbf1$ contains $0$ since $m$
contains $1$. Thus $h_{p-1}\partial E_m=E_m$.
In degree zero the units whose indices omit $0$ span $\eta_0(B)$,
on which both terms vanish. Hence $\partial h+h\partial=1-\eta\epsilon$.
If some $c_a=0$, then $B=0$ and $h$ contracts $N(X)$.
If every $c_a>0$, the retraction onto $B[0]$ gives $H_0(X)\cong B\ne0$.
The coordinate bases of $N_p(X)$, $Z_p$ and
$R_{p,j}(X)$ are indexed independently of $A$, so these modules commute
with arbitrary base change.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ranks}
In the unaugmented complex every $0$-chain is a cycle. Put $b_{-1}=0$ and
let $b_p=\rank Z_p$ be its boundary rank in degree $p\geq0$. Then
\[
 b_p=\sum_{t=0}^{p+1}(-1)^t\binom{p+1}{t}
          \prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t),\qquad
 \rank N_p(X)=b_p+b_{p-1}+\mathbf 1_{p=0}\prod_a c_a.
\]
For $c_a=0$ on all axes, $b_p=(p+1)!\SII{k}{p+1}$, where
$\SII{k}{r}$ is a Stirling number of the second kind.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Excluding $t$ prescribed indices of $[p]$ leaves $p+1+c_a-t$ choices on
axis $a$. Inclusion--exclusion in \eqref{eq:cycles} gives $b_p$, and
\eqref{eq:disks} gives $\rank N_p(X)$. When every $c_a=0$, tuples covering $[p]$
are surjections from a $k$-element set onto $[p]$
\cite[\S1.4]{StanleyEC1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For matrices with $c_1=c_2=0$,
$N_\bullet(X)\cong D^1(A)\oplus D^2(A^2)$.
The normalized ranks are $1,3,2$ in degrees $0,1,2$. In degree one,
$d_0E_{(1,1)}=E_{(0,0)}$. In degree two, $d_0$ sends $E_{(1,2)}$
and $E_{(2,1)}$ to $E_{(0,1)}$ and $E_{(1,0)}$, respectively.
Over a field, $h_1E_{01}=E_{12}$ lies outside the submodule generated
by $E_{01}$ (Example~\ref{ex:matrix-cycle}).
The function \program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L164}{square_matrix_example} computes these ranks and images from the faces.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:shape}
Over a fixed nonzero commutative ring $A$, $X^{\mathbf c}(A)\cong X^{\mathbf c'}(A)$
if and only if the entries of $\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf c'$ agree up to permutation.
Two shapes placed in their simplicial dimensions determine isomorphic
ambient modules if and only if one is obtained from the other by permuting
the axes and adding the same integer to every axis length.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Reducing an isomorphism modulo a maximal ideal of $A$ gives equal
dimensions in every degree. Hence the rank polynomials
$\prod_a(x+c_a+1)$ and $\prod_a(x+c'_a+1)$ agree at every nonnegative
integer and are equal in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Their roots determine the
multiset of integers $c_a$. Conversely, permuting the axes induces
the required simplicial isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{Chain contractions and ordinal sums}\label{sec:contractions}
Inserting a zero on each axis gives a chain homotopy before normalization.
For $A$-modules $E,W$, let $V_A(E,W)$ be the \v{C}ech nerve of the split
projection $E\oplus W\to W$ \cite[Tag 019P]{StacksCech}. In degree $p$
it is $E^{p+1}\oplus W$. Faces delete and degeneracies repeat
$E$-coordinates, fixing $W$. Form $V=\bigotimes_a V_A(E_a,W_a)$ over
$A$ degreewise. The projections and zero sections give
$\epsilon:V\to\underline B$ and $\eta:\underline B\to V$, where
$B=\bigotimes_a W_a$. With $E_a=A$ and $W_a=A^{c_a}$ the coordinate
bases identify $V$ with $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$.

Let $H_p:V_p\to V_{p+1}$ insert a zero before the first $E_a$-coordinate in each factor.
With $H_{-1}=\eta_0$ and $d_0=\epsilon_0$ on $V_0$, deleting the
inserted coordinate gives $d_0H_p=1$. Deleting any other gives
$d_iH_p=H_{p-1}d_{i-1}$ for $1\leq i\leq p+1$.
On unaugmented chains the alternating sum gives $\partial H+H\partial=1$
in positive degrees and $\partial_1H_0=1-\eta_0\epsilon_0$ in degree
zero. Thus $\epsilon_0$ and $\eta_0$, extended by zero in positive
degrees, are chain homotopy inverses between this complex and $B[0]$.
Projection along $D_{p+1}(V)$ gives the homotopy on $N(V)$.
For excesses computed from a shape, some $c_a=0$, so $B=0$
and the chain complex of $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$ is contractible. For square matrices,
\program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L164}{square_matrix_example} checks $\partial H+H\partial=1$ on every matrix unit in degrees zero through two.

For $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$, $H_pE_m=E_{m+\mathbf1}$. If $E_m\in N_p(X)$,
its image is normalized when $0\in\im(m)$ and equals $s_0E_m$
otherwise. The projection thus gives the homotopy of
Section~\ref{sec:normalized}. Axis permutations preserving $\mathbf c$
commute with both homotopies. Hence the homotopies preserve the fixed
submodule and the submodule on which these permutations act by their signs.
On square complex matrices they commute with conjugate transpose over $\mathbb R$.

Suppose some $c_a=0$. For $t\in\mathbb Z$ let $F^tX_p$ be the span
of the $E_m$ with $\min_a m_a\geq p+t$. A nonzero face lowers the
minimum by at most one, and $H_p$ raises it by one. Thus $\partial$
and $H$ preserve this decreasing filtration, finite in each degree,
and $H$ contracts each quotient $F^tX_\bullet/F^{t+1}X_\bullet$.
The homology groups of these quotients form the $E^1$-page of the
associated spectral sequence, so $E^1=0$.

Adjoin the empty ordinal $[-1]$ to $\Delta$ to obtain $\Delta_+$.
Let $X^+$ denote $X$ augmented by $\epsilon_0:X_0\to B$.
For $p,q\geq-1$ put
\[
 I_{p,q}=\prod_a\bigl([p]\oplus[c_a-1]\oplus[q]\bigr),\qquad
 Y_{p,q}=A^{I_{p,q}}.
\]
For $\alpha,\beta$ in $\Delta_+$, the operator $Y(\alpha,\beta)$ acts by precomposition
with $\prod_a(\alpha\oplus1_{[c_a-1]}\oplus\beta)$. Exchanging the last two
summands as sets identifies $Y_{p,q}$ with $X^+_{p+q+1}$ and $Y(\alpha,\beta)$
with $X^+(\alpha\oplus\beta)$. At fixed $q$, the summand $[q]$ contributes $q+1$ indices
on each axis, so the excess on axis $a$ of the horizontal row is $c_a+q+1$.
Substituting in Proposition~\ref{prop:disks} gives the unaugmented row homology
$A^{\prod_a(c_a+q+1)}$ in degree zero and zero in positive degrees.

Include the horizontal augmentation $Y_{0,q}\to Y_{-1,q}$. For
$p\geq-1$, extension by zero along the coface $[p]\to[p+1]$ skipping $0$
in the first ordinal summand on each axis gives $H_{p,q}:Y_{p,q}\to Y_{p+1,q}$. Then
$\partial_hH+H\partial_h=1$, and $H$ commutes with $\partial_v$ since
vertical maps act only on $[q]$. For the total differential
$\partial=\partial_h+(-1)^p\partial_v$ the vertical terms in
$\partial H+H\partial$ cancel, so $H$ contracts the total complex.
