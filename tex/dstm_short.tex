\begin{abstract}
We compute the homology of the simplicial module generated over a field $K$
by a hypermatrix $T$ of shape $(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ and simplicial dimension
$n=\min_a n_a-1$. Faces delete the same index on every axis,
and degeneracies repeat it. Restrict each axis of $T$ to the same $p+1$
indices in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and all its indices above $n$. The projections
of these restrictions span the degree-$p$ normalized chains of the generated module.
The Python package \emph{SimplicialTensors} computes its Betti numbers over
$\mathbb Q$ and finite prime fields. The full simplicial module determined by the shape has a contractible chain complex. For $q\geq1$,
the least number of axes on which a submodule can have nonzero $q$th homology is $q+1$.
Order the vertices of a finite simple graph on at least two vertices and
insert one isolated vertex between each successive pair. The mod-$2$
boundary of the adjacency matrix in this order has full rank exactly when
the original order traces a Hamiltonian path. Counting such orders recovers
$22{,}394{,}880$ oriented Hamiltonian paths for the $4\times4$ rook graph
and gives $22{,}230{,}528$ for the cospectral Shrikhande graph.
Each tensor induces a map from the normalized chains $C$ of the standard
$n$-simplex to the ambient normalized complex $\mathcal D$. A subcomplex
$L\subseteq C$ occurs as a kernel precisely when the cycle and boundary
dimensions of $C/L$ do not exceed those of $\mathcal D$ in any degree.
Two quotients are isomorphic exactly when a chain automorphism of $C$
carries one kernel to the other. Over an infinite field the kernels of
minimal dimension in each degree have rational Zariski closure in a product
of Grassmannians. In the dendroidal extension on $k$ axes, horns fill uniquely
on trees with at least $k+2$ vertices. This bound is sharp for inner horns if $k\geq2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The shape of a hypermatrix lists its axis lengths. Its simplicial dimension
is one less than the shortest length. The face $d_i$ deletes index $i$ on
every axis, and the degeneracy $s_i$ repeats it. Inserting a zero slice
on each axis contracts the chain complex of the module defined by the shape
(Section~\ref{sec:contractions}). Over a field $K$, the $2\times2$ matrix
unit $E_{01}$ generates a submodule with one-dimensional first homology
(Example~\ref{ex:matrix-cycle}).

For a degree-$n$ hypermatrix $T$ on $k$ axes, each choice of $p+1$ indices
in $\{0,\ldots,n\}$ gives a restriction of $T$ that retains all additional
indices on each axis. Projecting these restrictions to normalized chains
gives a matrix with $\binom{n+1}{p+1}$ columns. Only degrees
$0\leq p\leq\min(n,k)$ contribute. The Python package \emph{SimplicialTensors}
computes the Betti numbers from the ranks of the columns and of their
boundaries in each degree (Section~\ref{sec:generated}). The source code and examples are at
\url{https://github.com/flengyel/SimplicialTensors} \cite{SimplicialTensors}.

Three-axis tensors realize the incidence complex of a finite graph
(Proposition~\ref{prop:graph}). For a simple graph $H$ on $n\geq2$ vertices, insert one isolated vertex
between each successive pair in a vertex order $\pi$ (Figure~\ref{fig:interleaved-path}).
Over $\mathbb F_2$ the boundary of the adjacency matrix in this order has
rank $2n-2$ exactly when $\pi$ traces a Hamiltonian path in $H$. Rank~$30$ occurs in $22{,}394{,}880$
orders of the $4\times4$ rook graph and $22{,}230{,}528$ orders of the
cospectral Shrikhande graph. The rook count agrees with the number of oriented
Hamiltonian paths in OEIS A096970 \cite{OEISRook}. In general the maximum rank
is $2(n-p)$, where $p$ is the minimum number of vertex-disjoint paths
covering the graph (Proposition~\ref{prop:ordered-graph-ranks}).

Example~\ref{ex:parameter-jump} gives an integral $3\times3$ matrix whose
generated submodule has first Betti number one over $\mathbb Q$ and zero
over $\mathbb F_3$. For $q\geq1$, the degree-$q$ basis tensor
at $(0,1,\ldots,q)$ on $q+1$ axes generates a submodule whose normalized
complex is $K$ concentrated in degree $q$. On fewer axes every submodule
has zero $q$th homology (Example~\ref{ex:sparse-higher-cycle}).

Let $C$ be the normalized chains of the standard $n$-simplex.
Two degree-$n$ tensors have the same kernel in $C$ exactly when an
isomorphism of their generated modules takes one tensor to the other.
After forgetting the generators, the isomorphism classes are the orbits of
kernels under chain automorphisms of $C$. Every isomorphism of the quotient
complexes lifts to such an automorphism (Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift}).
A subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ occurs as a kernel precisely when the cycle and
boundary dimensions of $C/L$ are at most those of the ambient normalized
complex in every degree (Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel-realizability}).
Where all ranks are maximal, the kernels define a map to a product of
Grassmannians. If $K$ is infinite, the closure of its image is rational
over $K$. For $3\times3$ matrices the image is a projective plane minus
one point, and its $K$-points form one orbit (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-families}).

In the full simplicial tensor module on $k$ axes, horns have unique fillers
precisely in degrees $p>k$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis}). In its extension
to rooted trees, every compatible family on all but one elementary face
extends uniquely on trees with at least $k+2$ vertices. For $k\geq2$,
restriction to an inner horn on $k+1$ vertices can have cokernel free of
rank $k!$. On one axis every inner horn fills uniquely (Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-cutoff}).

\section{The construction}
Throughout, $A$ is a nonzero commutative ring with identity, $k\geq1$, and
$[p]=\{0,\ldots,p\}$ for $p\geq0$. Let $\Delta$ denote the category of finite nonempty
ordinals and nondecreasing morphisms.

Fix a \emph{shape} $\vec s=(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ of positive integer axis lengths. Its \emph{simplicial dimension} is $n(\vec s)=\min_a n_a-1$.
Put $c_a=n_a-1-n(\vec s)$ and $I_p=\prod_{a=1}^k[p+c_a]$.
In simplicial degree $p$, $c_a$ is the number of indices on axis $a$
strictly greater than $p$. Those indices are $p+1,\ldots,p+c_a$.
In degree $n(\vec s)$ the hypermatrices have shape $\vec s$.
A $3\times5$ matrix has simplicial dimension $2$ and additional column indices $3,4$.

With $[-1]$ the empty ordinal, put $J_c([p])=[p]\oplus[c-1]=[p+c]$
for $c\geq0$, where $\oplus$ is the ordinal sum \cite[Ch.~VII, \S5, p.~175]{MacLaneCategories}.
For $\alpha:[p]\to[q]$,
$J_c(\alpha)=\alpha\oplus 1_{[c-1]}$ sends $i\in[p]$
to $\alpha(i)$ and $p+t$ to $q+t$ for $1\leq t\leq c$. Thus $J_c:\Delta\to\mathbf{Set}$ is a functor and
$I=\prod_a J_{c_a}$ is a cosimplicial set. A hypermatrix in degree $p$
is a function $I_p\to A$. The \emph{diagonal simplicial tensor module}
$X(\vec s;A):\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_A$ is given by
\[
 X_p(\vec s;A)=A^{I_p},\qquad
 X(\alpha):X_q\longrightarrow X_p,\quad T\longmapsto T\circ I(\alpha).
\]
The standard basis of $X_p$ consists of the functions $E_m$ equal
to $1$ at $m\in I_p$ and $0$ elsewhere.

The same formulas define $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$ for any nonnegative integers $c_a$.
For $c_a=n_a-1-n(\vec s)$ we have $X^{\mathbf c}(A)=X(\vec s;A)$ and $\min_a c_a=0$.
In general the shape in degree $p$ is $(p+1+c_a)_a$, of simplicial dimension
$p+\min_a c_a$. Such shifts occur in the ordinal sums of Section~\ref{sec:contractions}.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the index coface
$\Delta_i:I_{p-1}\to I_p$ skips $i$ in every coordinate.
For $p\geq0$ and $0\leq i\leq p$, the codegeneracy
$\Sigma_i:I_{p+1}\to I_p$ identifies $i$ and $i+1$ in every coordinate. Thus $d_iT=T\circ\Delta_i$ and
$s_iT=T\circ\Sigma_i$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:face}
 d_iE_m=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&i\in\im(m),\\
 E_{\Delta_i^{-1}m},&i\notin\im(m),
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \im(m)=\{m_1,\ldots,m_k\}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $i$, $\Delta_i$ maps $I_{p-1}$ bijectively onto the tuples avoiding $i$.
For any simplicial module $Y$, write $H_p(Y)$ for the homology of its
unaugmented chain complex, with differential
$\partial_p=\sum_{i=0}^p(-1)^i d_i$ for $p\geq1$ and $\partial_0=0$.
On one axis, $\partial_1(3,5)=(5)-(3)=(2)$.

In multidegree $(p_1,\ldots,p_k)$ take the module $A^{\prod_a[p_a+c_a]}$.
Pullback in each axis gives a $k$-fold simplicial module.
Its restriction along the diagonal $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})^k$ is $X$.
For $k=2$, this is the diagonal used by Quillen for double
semisimplicial groups \cite{Quillen1966}. The finite coordinate bases identify
$X^{\mathbf c}(A)\otimes_A B$ with $X^{\mathbf c}(B)$ for any ring
homomorphism $A\to B$.

For $T\in X_n$, the simplicial submodule $\langle T\rangle$ generated by
$T$ has degree-$p$ term spanned by $X(\alpha)(T)$ for $\alpha:[p]\to[n]$.

\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-cycle}
Over a field $K$, take shape $(2,2)$, write $E_{ij}=E_{(i,j)}$ and let
\[
 T=E_{01}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad Y=\langle T\rangle\subseteq X.
\]
Both faces vanish, so $Y_0=0$, $Y_1=KT$, and
\[
 Y_2=\operatorname{span}_K\{s_0T,s_1T\},\qquad
 s_0T=E_{02}+E_{12},\quad s_1T=E_{01}+E_{02}.
\]
Both degeneracies have zero boundary by the simplicial identities.
Thus $H_1(Y)\cong K$. In the ambient complex, $\partial_2E_{12}=T$.
Every matrix in $Y_2$ satisfies $x_{02}=x_{12}+x_{01}$, so $E_{12}\notin Y_2$.
\end{example}

The script \path{dstm_examples.py} computes this homology over $\mathbb Q$
and the free coordinates in Example~\ref{ex:matrix-horn}.

\section{Horn kernels and normalization}
For a simplicial $A$-module $Y$, the normalized and degenerate chains in
degree $p$ are, respectively,
\[
 N_p(Y)=\bigcap_{i=1}^p\ker d_i,\qquad
 D_p(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\im s_i.
\]
Here $N_0(Y)=Y_0$ and $D_0(Y)=0$. The differential on $N(Y)$ is
$d_0$ in positive degrees and zero in degree zero
\cite[Exercise 8.3.4]{Weibel}. Reversing the face and degeneracy indices
in Dold's splitting \cite[Corollary 1.12]{Dold1958} gives
$Y_p=N_p(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$. The inclusion of normalized chains
induces $H_p(N(Y))\cong H_p(Y)$ \cite[Theorem 8.3.8]{Weibel}.
Normalization gives the Dold--Kan equivalence with nonnegative chain
complexes \cite[Theorem 8.4.1]{Weibel}.

For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$, let $R_{p,j}(Y)=
\bigcap_{0\leq i\leq p,\ i\ne j}\ker d_i$.
The module $\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$ of compatible horns consists of tuples
$(y_i)_{i\ne j}$ with $y_i\in Y_{p-1}$ satisfying $d_i y_\ell=d_{\ell-1}y_i$
for $i<\ell$, $i,\ell\ne j$. This condition is vacuous for $p=1$.
The horn map is
$\Phi_j:Y_p\to\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$, $y\mapsto(d_i y)_{i\ne j}$.
Every simplicial module is Kan, so $\Phi_j$ is surjective
\cite[Lemma 8.2.8]{Weibel}. If $x$ fills a horn, its fillers form the
coset $x+R_{p,j}(Y)$.

Ashley's theorem on horn fillers in $D_p(Y)$ applies to the underlying
simplicial abelian group \cite[Ch.~3, Theorem~1.5]{Ashley1978}.
For simplicial vector spaces see Ronchi and Zhu \cite[Lemma~2.10]{RonchiZhu}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hornsplit}
Let $Y$ be a simplicial $A$-module, $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$.
Then $Y_p=R_{p,j}(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$, and the horn map restricts to an
isomorphism $D_p(Y)\to\Lambda_{p,j}(Y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\leq j<p$, the simplicial identities give inverse maps
\[
 F_j=1-s_jd_j:R_{p,j}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j+1}(Y),\qquad
 G_j=1-s_jd_{j+1}:R_{p,j+1}(Y)\longrightarrow R_{p,j}(Y).
\]
The faces of $s_jd_jx$ for $x\in R_{p,j}(Y)$ vanish away
from $j,j+1$. For $i<j$ use $d_id_j=d_{j-1}d_i$, and for
$i>j+1$ use $d_{i-1}d_j=d_jd_i$ after moving $d_i$ past $s_j$.
The identities $d_js_j=d_{j+1}s_j=1$ give $d_jF_jx=0$ and
$d_{j+1}F_jx=-d_jx$, so $G_jF_jx=x$. For $y\in R_{p,j+1}(Y)$ the
same face identities applied to $s_jd_{j+1}y$ give
$d_{j+1}G_jy=0$ and $d_jG_jy=-d_{j+1}y$, so $F_jG_jy=y$.
Both maps induce the identity modulo $D_p(Y)$. Since
$R_{p,0}(Y)=N_p(Y)$ maps isomorphically onto $Y_p/D_p(Y)$,
the same holds for every $R_{p,j}(Y)$. Since $\Phi_j$ is surjective with
kernel $R_{p,j}(Y)$, its restriction to $D_p(Y)$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hornbasis}
Let $X=X^{\mathbf c}(A)$, $p\geq1$, and $0\leq j\leq p$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hornbasis}
 R_{p,j}(X)=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\{}[p]\setminus\{j\}\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m,
 \qquad
 \rank R_{p,j}(X)=
 \sum_{t=0}^p(-1)^t\binom pt\prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t).
\end{equation}
Consequently, every horn has more than one filler for $p\leq k$ and a
unique filler for $p>k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:face}, $d_iT=0$ exactly when $T$ is supported on
tuples containing $i$. Intersecting these support conditions gives
the basis in \eqref{eq:hornbasis}. To count its elements by
inclusion--exclusion, forbid $t$ of the $p$ required labels.
This leaves $p+c_a+1-t$ choices in coordinate $a$.
A tuple of length $k$ cannot contain $p>k$ distinct values. If
$p\leq k$, assign the $p$ required values to distinct coordinates
and fill the remaining coordinates arbitrarily in $[p]$.
This gives a basis element since $A\ne0$.
\end{proof}


\begin{example}\label{ex:matrix-horn}
For shape $(3,3)$, take the $2$-horn with missing face $0$ and prescribed
faces $y_1,y_2$ below. They agree in their upper left entry, and their
fillers are the displayed matrices $T$ as $u,v$ range over $A$.
\[
 y_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\7&9\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 y_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\4&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&3\\4&5&u\\7&v&9\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The unique filler in $D_2(X)$ is $s_1y_2+s_0(y_1-y_2)$, which gives
$u=6$ and $v=8$. The matrix units $E_{(1,2)}$ and $E_{(2,1)}$
form a basis of $R_{2,0}(X)$.
\end{example}

For $r\geq0$, a simplicial $A$-module is an $r$-hypergroupoid if its
underlying Kan complex has unique horn fillers in dimensions greater
than $r$ \cite{Duskin1979,Glenn1982}. By Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis},
the least such $r$ for $X$ is $k$.
For a shape $\vec s$ of simplicial dimension $n$, the $n$-hypergroupoid
condition is $k\leq n$.

\section{The normalized complex}\label{sec:normalized}
Put $C_a=\{1,\ldots,c_a\}$ and $B=A^{\prod_a C_a}$. Restriction to
the tuples $(p+t_1,\ldots,p+t_k)$ with $t_a\in C_a$ defines a simplicial
morphism $\epsilon:X\to\underline B$ to the constant module on $B$. Extension by zero gives a
section $\eta$. Let $Z_p$ denote the cycles of $N(X)$ augmented by
$\epsilon_0:X_0\to B$. In degree zero $Z_0=\ker\epsilon_0$ is spanned by
the $E_m$ with $0\in\im(m)$. In positive degrees cycles lie in the kernel of
every face. Hence \eqref{eq:face} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
 Z_p=\bigoplus_{\substack{m\in I_p\\{}[p]\subseteq\im(m)}} AE_m
 \qquad(p\geq0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:disks}
Let $X=X^{\mathbf c}(A)$. For $p\geq1$ and $0\leq j\leq p$, the sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow Z_p\longrightarrow R_{p,j}(X)
 \xrightarrow{d_j} Z_{p-1}\longrightarrow0
\]
is split exact, with section $\iota_j$ of $d_j$ given by extension by
zero along $\Delta_j$.
If $D^r(M)$ denotes the identity complex $M\xrightarrow{1}M$
in degrees $r,r-1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disks}
 N_\bullet(X)\cong B[0]\oplus\bigoplus_{p=0}^{k-1}D^{p+1}(Z_p).
\end{equation}
In particular $H_0(X)\cong A^{\prod_a c_a}$ and $H_p(X)=0$ for $p>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Partition the basis of $R_{p,j}(X)$ according as the tuple contains
$j$ or avoids it. The tuples containing $j$ give the basis of $Z_p$.
Deleting the gap at $j$ sends the other tuples bijectively to the
basis of $Z_{p-1}$ by \eqref{eq:face}, with inverse $\iota_j$.
For $j=0$ this gives $N_p(X)=Z_p\oplus\iota_0Z_{p-1}$, with $d_0$
zero on $Z_p$ and inverse to $\iota_0$ on the second summand. Since
$X_0=\eta_0(B)\oplus Z_0$, the remaining summand is $B[0]$.
The sum ends at $p=k-1$ because a tuple of length $k$ cannot contain
every index in $[p]$ for $p\geq k$.
\end{proof}

The chain homotopy $h$ sends each basis element $E_m$ of $Z_p$ to
$\iota_0 E_m=E_{m+\mathbf1}$ and is zero on the complementary summand.
Thus $\partial h+h\partial=1-\eta\epsilon$ on normalized chains.
It follows that $N(X)$ retracts onto $B[0]$ and is contractible precisely
when some $c_a=0$. The coordinate bases of $N_p(X)$, $Z_p$ and
$R_{p,j}(X)$ are indexed independently of $A$, so these modules commute
with arbitrary base change.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:ranks}
Let $b_p=\rank Z_p$, the rank of the degree-$p$ boundaries of $N(X)$,
and put $b_{-1}=0$. For $p\geq0$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 b_p=\sum_{t=0}^{p+1}(-1)^t\binom{p+1}{t}
          \prod_{a=1}^k(p+c_a+1-t),\\
 \rank N_p(X)=b_p+b_{p-1}+\mathbf 1_{p=0}\prod_a c_a.
 \end{gathered}
\]
For $c_a=0$ on all axes, $b_p=(p+1)!\SII{k}{p+1}$, where
$\SII{k}{r}$ is a Stirling number of the second kind.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply inclusion--exclusion to \eqref{eq:cycles} and use
\eqref{eq:disks}. When every $c_a=0$, tuples covering $[p]$
are surjections from a $k$-element set onto $[p]$
\cite[\S1.4]{StanleyEC1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
For matrices with $c_1=c_2=0$,
$N_\bullet(X)\cong D^1(A)\oplus D^2(A^2)$.
The normalized ranks are $1,3,2$ in degrees $0,1,2$. In degree one,
$d_0E_{(1,1)}=E_{(0,0)}$. In degree two, $d_0$ sends $E_{(1,2)}$
and $E_{(2,1)}$ to $E_{(0,1)}$ and $E_{(1,0)}$, respectively.
\end{example}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:shape}
Over a fixed nonzero commutative ring $A$, $X(\vec s;A)\cong X(\vec s';A)$
if and only if $\vec s'$ is obtained from $\vec s$ by permuting the axis lengths
and adding a common integer. More generally, $X^{\mathbf c}(A)\cong X^{\mathbf c'}(A)$
if and only if the entries of $\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf c'$ agree up to permutation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Reducing an isomorphism modulo a maximal ideal of $A$ gives equal
dimensions in every degree. Hence the rank polynomials
$\prod_a(x+c_a+1)$ and $\prod_a(x+c'_a+1)$ agree at every nonnegative
integer and are equal in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Their roots determine the
multiset of integers $c_a$. Conversely, permuting the axes induces
the required simplicial isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{Chain contractions and ordinal sums}\label{sec:contractions}
Inserting a zero on each axis gives a chain homotopy before normalization.
For $A$-modules $E,W$, let $V_A(E,W)$ be the \v{C}ech nerve of the split
projection $E\oplus W\to W$ \cite[Tag 019P]{StacksCech}. In degree $p$
it is $E^{p+1}\oplus W$. Faces delete and degeneracies repeat
$E$-coordinates, fixing $W$. Form $V=\bigotimes_a V_A(E_a,W_a)$ over
$A$ degreewise. The projections and zero sections give
$\epsilon:V\to\underline B$ and $\eta:\underline B\to V$, where
$B=\bigotimes_a W_a$. With $E_a=A$ and $W_a=A^{c_a}$ the coordinate
bases identify $V$ with $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$.

Let $H_p:V_p\to V_{p+1}$ insert a zero before the first $E_a$-coordinate in each factor.
With $H_{-1}=\eta_0$ and $d_0=\epsilon_0$ on $V_0$, deleting the
inserted coordinate gives $d_0H_p=1$. Deleting any other gives
$d_iH_p=H_{p-1}d_{i-1}$ for $1\leq i\leq p+1$.
On unaugmented chains the alternating sum gives $\partial H+H\partial=1$
in positive degrees and $\partial_1H_0=1-\eta_0\epsilon_0$ in degree
zero. Thus $\epsilon_0$ and $\eta_0$, extended by zero in positive
degrees, are chain homotopy inverses between this complex and $B[0]$.
Projection along $D_{p+1}(V)$ gives the homotopy on $N(V)$.
For every shape $\vec s$, some $c_a=n_a-1-n(\vec s)$ is zero, so $B=0$
and the chain complex of $X(\vec s;A)$ is contractible.

For $X^{\mathbf c}(A)$, $H_pE_m=E_{m+\mathbf1}$. If $E_m\in N_p(X)$,
its image is normalized when $0\in\im(m)$ and equals $s_0E_m$
otherwise. The projection thus gives the homotopy of
Section~\ref{sec:normalized}. Axis permutations preserving $\mathbf c$
commute with both homotopies. Hence the homotopies preserve the fixed
submodule and the submodule on which these permutations act by their signs.
On square complex matrices they commute with conjugate transpose over $\mathbb R$.

Suppose some $c_a=0$. For $t\in\mathbb Z$ let $F^tX_p$ be the span
of the $E_m$ with $\min_a m_a\geq p+t$. A nonzero face lowers the
minimum by at most one, and $H_p$ raises it by one. Thus $\partial$
and $H$ preserve this decreasing filtration, finite in each degree,
and $H$ contracts each quotient $F^tX_\bullet/F^{t+1}X_\bullet$.

Adjoin the empty ordinal $[-1]$ to $\Delta$ to obtain $\Delta_+$.
Let $X^+$ denote $X$ augmented by $\epsilon_0:X_0\to B$.
For $p,q\geq-1$ put
\[
 I_{p,q}=\prod_a\bigl([p]\oplus[c_a-1]\oplus[q]\bigr),\qquad
 Y_{p,q}=A^{I_{p,q}}.
\]
For $\alpha,\beta$ in $\Delta_+$, pull back along
$\alpha\oplus1_{[c_a-1]}\oplus\beta$ on each axis. Exchanging the last two
summands as sets identifies $Y_{p,q}$ with $X^+_{p+q+1}$ and the pullback
with $X^+(\alpha\oplus\beta)$. At fixed $q$, the unaugmented horizontal row
has parameters $c_a+q+1$, so its homology is $A^{\prod_a(c_a+q+1)}$ in
degree zero and zero in positive degrees (Proposition~\ref{prop:disks}).

Include the horizontal augmentation $Y_{0,q}\to Y_{-1,q}$. For
$p\geq-1$, extension by zero along the coface $[p]\to[p+1]$ skipping $0$
in the first ordinal summand on each axis gives $H_{p,q}:Y_{p,q}\to Y_{p+1,q}$. Then
$\partial_hH+H\partial_h=1$, and $H$ commutes with $\partial_v$ since
vertical maps act only on $[q]$. For the total differential
$\partial=\partial_h+(-1)^p\partial_v$ the vertical terms in
$\partial H+H\partial$ cancel, so $H$ contracts the total complex.
