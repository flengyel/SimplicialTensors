\section{Generated submodules}
\label{sec:generated}
Fix a field $K$, $n\geq0$ and $\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k$.
Put $X=X^{\mathbf c}(K)$ and let $P=K[\Delta^n]$ be the free simplicial
$K$-module on the standard $n$-simplex. A basis of $P_p$ consists of
the nondecreasing maps $[p]\to[n]$.
For $T\in X_n$, the representing property of $P$ gives
\[
 f_T:P_\bullet\longrightarrow X_\bullet,
 \qquad f_{T,p}(\alpha)=X(\alpha)(T)
 \quad\bigl(\alpha:[p]\to[n]\bigr).
\]
Its image is the simplicial submodule $\langle T\rangle$ generated by $T$.
For any simplicial module $Y$, the projection $\nu_p:Y_p\to N_p(Y)$ is
\[
 \nu_0=1,\qquad
 \nu_p=(1-s_0d_1)\cdots(1-s_{p-1}d_p)\quad(p>0).
\]
Apply the factors from right to left. Each $1-s_{i-1}d_i$ has image in
$\ker d_i$, preserves $\ker d_j$ for $j>i$, and induces the identity
modulo $D_p(Y)$. The splitting $Y_p=N_p(Y)\oplus D_p(Y)$ gives the formula.
In $P_p$ the noninjective maps span $D_p(P)$, so the $\nu_p\alpha$
for increasing injections $\alpha:[p]\hookrightarrow[n]$ form a basis of $N_p(P)$.
Since $f_T$ commutes with faces and degeneracies, it sends this basis
to the columns $\nu_p X(\alpha)(T)$ spanning $N_p(\langle T\rangle)$.
Let $r_p(T)$ be their rank. For $p>0$ let $\delta_p(T)$ be the rank of their
images under $d_0$, and put $\delta_0(T)=0$.
The $p$th Betti number is $r_p(T)-\delta_p(T)-\delta_{p+1}(T)$.
Over $\mathbb Q$ or a finite prime field,
\program{src/simplicial_tensors/homology.py\#L132}{generated_homology} takes a hypermatrix in the simplicial dimension of its shape.
For a tensor with specified degree and excesses, use
\program{src/simplicial_tensors/tensor_chain_complex.py\#L130}{sparse_generated_homology}.
For $p>n$ there are no columns. For $p>k$, $N_p(X)=0$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:hornbasis}.

Put
\[
 C=N(P),\qquad g_T=N(f_T),\qquad L(T)=\ker g_T.
\]
Normalization is exact, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generated-normalization}
 N(\langle T\rangle)\cong C/L(T).
\end{equation}
Here $\dim C_p=\binom{n+1}{p+1}$ for $0\leq p\leq n$ and $C_p=0$ for $p>n$.
By Dold--Kan the subspaces $L_p(T)$ determine $\ker f_T$. For
$T,T'\in X_n$, $L(T)=L(T')$ holds precisely when there is a simplicial
isomorphism $\langle T\rangle\to\langle T'\rangle$ sending $T$ to $T'$.
In shape $(2,2)$ the generators $E_{01}$ and $E_{10}$ have the same
kernel ($L_0=C_0$, $L_1=0$) and span distinct lines in degree one
(\program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L54}{generated_homology_example}).

Since $H_0(C)=K$ and
$H_j(C)=0$ for $j>0$, the short exact sequence
$0\to L(T)\to C\to C/L(T)\to0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generated-homology}
 \begin{gathered}
 H_j(\langle T\rangle)\cong H_{j-1}(L(T))\qquad(j\geq2),\\
 0\longrightarrow H_1(\langle T\rangle)\longrightarrow H_0(L(T))
 \xrightarrow{\epsilon}K\longrightarrow H_0(\langle T\rangle)
 \longrightarrow0,
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is induced by the augmentation sending every vertex of
$\Delta^n$ to $1$.

\subsection{Quotients and automorphisms}

To classify the generated modules after forgetting their generators, it suffices
by Dold--Kan to classify the quotients $C/L(T)$ up to chain isomorphism.
Write $\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)$ for the category of chain complexes of
$K$-vector spaces concentrated in nonnegative degrees. A complex is finite
if the direct sum of its chain groups is finite-dimensional. For a complex $E$,
write
\[
 Z_p(E)=\ker(\partial_p:E_p\to E_{p-1}),\qquad
 B_p(E)=\im(\partial_{p+1}:E_{p+1}\to E_p),
 \qquad E_{-1}=0.
\]
Thus $Z_0(E)=E_0$. For a $K$-vector space $V$ and
$p\geq0$, let $S^p(V)$ be the complex with $V$ in degree $p$ and zero elsewhere. Choosing complements of boundaries
inside cycles, and of cycles inside each chain group, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-chain-splitting}
 E\cong\bigoplus_{p\geq0}S^p(H_p(E))
       \oplus\bigoplus_{p\geq1}D^p(B_{p-1}(E))
\end{equation}
for every finite complex $E$. The differential maps the chosen
complement of $Z_p(E)$ isomorphically onto $B_{p-1}(E)$.
Thus the homology dimensions and differential ranks determine the
isomorphism class of a finite complex. Their additivity gives cancellation
under direct sums.

For the normalized simplex complex, the dimensions of its chain groups
and its homology give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex-projective}
 C\cong S^0(K)\oplus\bigoplus_{p=1}^n
                   D^p\bigl(K^{\binom np}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Chain maps from $S^0(V)$ and $D^p(V)$ to $E$ correspond to linear
maps $V\to E_0$ and $V\to E_p$, respectively. Evaluation in each
degree and $\operatorname{Hom}_K(V,-)$ are exact, so these summands
are projective and hence so is $C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quotient-lift}
Fix a field $K$ and $n\geq0$, and let $C=N(K[\Delta^n])$.
For subcomplexes $L,M\subseteq C$, every chain isomorphism
$\varphi:C/L\to C/M$ lifts to a chain automorphism $u:C\to C$
such that the square commutes.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 C \arrow[r,"u"] \arrow[d,"\pi_L"'] & C \arrow[d,"\pi_M"]\\
 C/L \arrow[r,"\varphi"'] & C/M .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Here $\pi_L$ and $\pi_M$ are the quotient maps. In particular,
$C/L\cong C/M$ if and only if $u(L)=M$ for some
$u\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)}(C)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Projectivity supplies chain maps $f,g:C\to C$ with
$\pi_Mf=\varphi\pi_L$ and $\pi_Lg=\varphi^{-1}\pi_M$.
Put $h=gf$ and $h'=fg$. For sufficiently large $m$ their Fitting
decompositions give direct sums of subcomplexes
\[
 C=U\oplus V=\im h^m\oplus\ker h^m,\qquad
 C=U'\oplus V'=\im (h')^m\oplus\ker (h')^m.
\]
The identities $fh=h'f$ and $gh'=hg$ give $f(U)\subseteq U'$ and
$g(U')\subseteq U$. Since $gf$ is invertible on $U$, $f|_U$ is
injective. Since $U'=fg(U')\subseteq f(U)$, it maps onto $U'$.
Cancellation in \eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting} gives
an isomorphism $a:V\to V'$. From $\pi_L(gf)^m=\pi_L$ and
$\pi_M(fg)^m=\pi_M$ we get $V\subseteq L$ and $V'\subseteq M$.
The automorphism $u=f|_U\oplus a$ then satisfies $\pi_Mu=\varphi\pi_L$
on $U$, by the choice of $f$, and on $V$, where both sides vanish.
Taking kernels gives $u(L)=M$.
Conversely, such an automorphism induces an isomorphism of quotients.
\end{proof}

Complexes in degrees $0,\ldots,n$ are modules over the path algebra
of $n\to\cdots\to0$ modulo paths of length two. This algebra and its opposite are
finite-dimensional and hence semilocal of stable range one
\cite[Corollary~6.5]{Bass1964}. Applied to $\varphi\pi_L$ and $\pi_M$,
both with finitely generated projective source $C$, Canfell's theorem on right
equivalence of presentations \cite[p.~141]{Canfell1992} also gives the lift.

For $a\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)}(C)$,
let $\widetilde a:P\to P$ be its simplicial automorphism under Dold--Kan.
The tensor
\[
 T'=f_{T,n}\bigl(\widetilde a_n(\operatorname{id}_{[n]})\bigr)\in X_n
\]
satisfies $f_{T'}=f_T\widetilde a$ by the representing property of $P$.
Thus $\langle T'\rangle=\langle T\rangle$, and $L(T)\cdot a=L(T')=a^{-1}L(T)$
defines a right action on $\{L(T):T\in X_n\}$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift},
its orbits classify the generated modules up to simplicial isomorphism.
By \eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting}, the chain dimensions $r_p(T)$ and
differential ranks $\delta_p(T)$ determine this isomorphism class.
Since $C$ is finite and $\dim C_p$ bounds both ranks, there are finitely many orbits.
The \texttt{lifting} case of \program{experiments/kernel_sequences.py}{kernel_sequences.py}
constructs a lift and checks the commutative square in each degree.

\subsection{Realizable kernels}

Put $\mathcal D=N(X)$. A subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ is
\emph{realizable in $X$} if $L=L(T)$ for some $T\in X_n$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kernel-realizability}
Let $K$ be a field, $n\geq0$, $C=N(K[\Delta^n])$, and
$X=X^{\mathbf c}(K)$ with $\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k$.
For a subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ and $Q=C/L$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is realizable in $X$.
\item There is a chain monomorphism $Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal D=N(X)$.
\item For every $p\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realizability-inequalities}
 \dim_K Z_p(Q)\leq\dim_K Z_p(\mathcal D),\qquad
 \dim_K B_p(Q)\leq\dim_K B_p(\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $L=L(T)$, then $g_T$ induces an injection $Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal D$.
Such an injection restricts to injections on cycles and boundaries.

Conversely, the boundary inequality gives an injection
$B_p(Q)\hookrightarrow B_p(\mathcal D)$ in each degree. By the cycle
inequality, it extends to an injection $j_p:Z_p(Q)\hookrightarrow Z_p(\mathcal D)$.
Choose a complement $W_p$ of $Z_p(Q)$ in $Q_p$. For $p>0$,
$\partial_Q:W_p\to B_{p-1}(Q)$ is an isomorphism. Lift
$j_{p-1}\partial_Q|_{W_p}$ through
$\partial_{\mathcal D}:\mathcal D_p\to B_{p-1}(\mathcal D)$ to a
linear map $\ell_p:W_p\to\mathcal D_p$. Set
$i_p(z+w)=j_p(z)+\ell_p(w)$ and take $i_0=j_0$, since $W_0=0$.
For $p>0$ we have $\partial_{\mathcal D}i_p(z+w)=j_{p-1}\partial_Qw
=i_{p-1}\partial_Q(z+w)$, so $i$ is a chain map. If $i_p(z+w)=0$,
this identity gives $w=0$ and then $z=0$. Since $i_0=j_0$ is injective,
$i:Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal D$ is a chain monomorphism.

Under Dold--Kan the composite $C\to Q\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal D$
corresponds to $f_T:P\to X$, where $T$ is the image of the identity
$n$-simplex. The kernel of the composite is $L$, so $L=L(T)$.
\end{proof}

For $p>0$, the cycles and boundaries of $\mathcal D$ coincide and have
dimension $b_p$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:ranks}. In degree zero,
\[
 \dim_K Z_0(\mathcal D)=\prod_a(c_a+1),\qquad
 \dim_K B_0(\mathcal D)=\prod_a(c_a+1)-\prod_a c_a.
\]
Since $B_p(Q)\subseteq Z_p(Q)$, the boundary inequality adds a condition
only in degree zero and only if every $c_a>0$. In particular, the cycle
inequalities suffice when the excesses are computed from a shape in its simplicial dimension.

For example, when $\mathbf c=(1,1)$, the cycle dimensions
of $\mathcal D$ are $(4,2,0)$ and its boundary dimensions are $(3,2,0)$
(\program{tex/anc/dstm_examples.py\#L195}{shifted_matrix_example}).
The complex $Q=D^1(K^4)$ is a quotient of $N(K[\Delta^4])$ by
\eqref{eq:simplex-projective}. It satisfies the cycle inequalities,
but $\dim B_0(Q)=4>3=\dim B_0(\mathcal D)$, so it does not embed.
The \texttt{obstruction} case of \program{experiments/kernel_sequences.py}{kernel_sequences.py}
checks this inequality. The \texttt{realize} case constructs a tensor from a prescribed kernel.

\subsection{Matrix examples and homology in higher degrees}

\begin{example}\label{ex:parameter-jump}
For $\mathbf c=(0,0)$ and $t\in K$, let
\[
 T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&1&1\\1&1&t\end{pmatrix}\in X_2,
 \qquad Y_t=\langle T(t)\rangle.
\]
Use the ordered basis $(E_{01},E_{10},E_{11})$ of $N_1(X)$.
For the restriction $y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&t\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
to vertices $1,2$, we have $\nu_1y=y-s_0(1)=(t-1)E_{11}$.
The three normalized restrictions are
\[
 v_{01}=(1,0,1),\qquad v_{02}=(0,1,t),\qquad
 v_{12}=(0,0,t-1).
\]
The matrix with these columns has determinant $t-1$. The normalized image of the top simplex
is $E_{12}-E_{21}$, whose differential is $E_{01}-E_{10}$.
The differential $N_1(X)\to K$ sends $(a,b,c)$ to $c$.
Thus the differential ranks of the generated complex are $(0,1,1)$,
indexed by source degree, and
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &(\dim N_0(Y_t),\dim N_1(Y_t),\dim N_2(Y_t))&(\dim H_0(Y_t),\dim H_1(Y_t),\dim H_2(Y_t))\\ \hline
 t\ne1&(1,3,1)&(0,1,0)\\
 t=1&(1,2,1)&(0,0,0).
\end{array}
\]
For $t\ne1$, the class of $E_{01}=v_{01}-(t-1)^{-1}v_{12}$
generates $H_1(Y_t)$.
At $t=1$, the cycle space in degree one is the line spanned by
$v_{01}-v_{02}=E_{01}-E_{10}$, which is already a boundary.

In the vertex basis of $C_0=K^3$, the degree-zero kernel is
\[
 L_0(T(t))=\ker(0,1,t)
   =\operatorname{span}_K\{(1,0,0),(0,-t,1)\}.
\]
These planes vary with $t$. For $t\ne1$, $L_1=L_2=0$, and the quotient
complexes have the same chain dimensions and differential ranks. Hence their kernels
lie in one orbit by Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift}. At $t=1$,
$L_1$ is spanned by $\alpha-s_0d_1\alpha$, where $\alpha:[1]\to[2]$
is the increasing injection with image $\{1,2\}$, and $L_2=0$. For example, $T(4)$ has
first Betti number one over $\mathbb Q$ and zero over $\mathbb F_3$.
The \texttt{jump} case of \program{experiments/kernel_sequences.py}{kernel_sequences.py}
computes the ranks at integral values of $t$ over either field.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The simplicial sphere]\label{ex:sparse-higher-cycle}
Fix $q\geq1$, $k=q+1$ and $\mathbf c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^k$,
and take $T_q=E_{(0,1,\ldots,q)}\in X^{\mathbf c}_q(K)$.
Every face of $T_q$ is zero by \eqref{eq:face}. Sending the top simplex to
$T_q$ therefore induces a surjection $K[\Delta^q]/K[\partial\Delta^q]\to
\langle T_q\rangle$. The source is the reduced free simplicial $K$-module on
the sphere $\Delta^q/\partial\Delta^q$. On normalized chains the surjection sends the sole generator,
in degree $q$, to the nonzero cycle $T_q$. It is therefore an isomorphism,
and Dold--Kan gives an isomorphism of simplicial modules. In particular,
\[
 N(\langle T_q\rangle)\cong S^q(K),\qquad
 H_q(\langle T_q\rangle)\cong K,\qquad
 H_p(\langle T_q\rangle)=0\quad(p\ne q).
\]
In the full module,
\[
 \partial_{q+1}E_{(1,2,\ldots,q+1)}=T_q.
\]
For $k\leq q$ no index tuple contains $[q]$, so \eqref{eq:cycles} gives
$Z_q(N(X))=0$. Hence $q+1$ is the least number of axes on which a simplicial
submodule can have nonzero $q$th homology.
\end{example}

For $\mathbf c=0$, the \texttt{spheres} case of
\program{experiments/kernel_sequences.py}{kernel_sequences.py} computes the
reduced Betti numbers. For the $2$-sphere it gives $(0,0,1)$ in degrees $0,1,2$.

\subsection{Encoding graph homology by tensors}

The $v$ vertices and $e$ edges of a finite graph $G$ are its $0$-cells
and $1$-cells. Its cellular chain complex over $A$ is the incidence complex
$A^e\to A^v$, so its homology is the ordinary graph homology $H_*(G;A)$.
Here $A$ is a nonzero commutative ring with identity, and $G$ may have
loops and parallel edges. Number the vertices and edges separately from $1$
and orient edge $j$ from $u_j$ to $w_j$.
Take $X=X^{(r,r,r)}(A)$ with $r\geq\max(v,e)$. Assign to each
vertex $t$ and each edge $j$ the tensors
\[
 a_t=E_{(0,t,t)}\in X_0,\qquad
 T_j=E_{(0,u_j+1,u_j+1)}+E_{(1,w_j+1,w_j+1)}
              +E_{(0,1,j+1)}\in X_1.
\]
Deleting index $1$ gives $d_1T_j=a_{u_j}$, and deleting index $0$ gives
$d_0T_j=a_{w_j}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:graph}
The simplicial submodule $Y_G$ generated by the $a_t$ and $T_j$
has normalized complex isomorphic to the incidence complex of $G$,
with edge $j$ having boundary $a_{w_j}-a_{u_j}$. Hence $H_*(Y_G)\cong H_*(G;A)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $a_t$ are distinct basis vectors of $X_0$. The normalized edges
$b_j=T_j-s_0a_{u_j}$ satisfy $d_0b_j=a_{w_j}-a_{u_j}$ and are linearly
independent because at $(0,1,j+1)$ only $b_j$ has a nonzero entry, equal to $1$.
In degree one the degeneracies of vertices project to zero. For $p>1$,
every map $[p]\to[0]$ or $[p]\to[1]$ factors through a codegeneracy,
so $N_p(Y_G)=0$.
\end{proof}

The generators in degrees $p=0,1$ use only first-axis indices in $[p]$.
Extension by zero on the additional first-axis indices commutes with simplicial
operators. Thus $Y_G$ lies in $X^{(0,r,r)}(A)$, of shape $(1,r+1,r+1)$ in degree
zero. The ambient complex $N(X^{(0,r,r)}(A))$ is contractible by Proposition~\ref{prop:disks}.

On each component, the boundaries of the edges of a spanning tree form
a basis of the kernel of the augmentation $a_t\mapsto 1$.
From each remaining edge subtract the signed sum of the edges on the tree
path from its source to its target, with signs given by the orientations.
These differences form a basis of the cycle module.
Thus $H_0(Y_G)\cong A^b$ and $H_1(Y_G)\cong A^{e-v+b}$, where $b$
is the number of components. The Petersen graph has ten vertices,
fifteen edges, and one component, so its first homology is $A^6$.
Over $\mathbb Q$,
\program{tex/anc/rank_shifted_homology.py}{rank_shifted_homology.py}
computes rank nine for the incidence map from the normalized tensor faces.

For a two-axis module $X'=X^{(c_1,c_2)}(A)$, the cycles in $N_1(X')$ have basis $E_{(0,1)}$ and
$E_{(1,0)}$ by \eqref{eq:cycles}. Over a field, every simplicial
submodule of $X'$ therefore has first Betti number at most two. In particular,
the Petersen incidence complex cannot occur on two axes over a field.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Hamiltonian paths and the mod-$2$ boundary]\label{prop:ordered-graph-ranks}
Let $H$ be a finite simple graph on $n\geq2$ vertices, with adjacency entries
$a_{uv}$. For a vertex order $\pi=(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})$, adjoin $n-1$ isolated
vertices to $H$ and let $A_\pi$ be the adjacency matrix in the order
$v_0,z_1,v_1,\ldots,z_{n-1},v_{n-1}$. Let $N_\pi$ be the adjacency matrix of $H$
with its vertices ordered by $\pi$ and its edges oriented from earlier to later.
Deleting each row and its matching column in turn and summing over $\mathbb F_2$
gives a boundary matrix $\partial A_\pi$ satisfying
\[
 \rank(\partial A_\pi)=2\rank N_\pi,\qquad
 \det(\partial A_\pi)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}a_{v_i v_{i+1}}.
\]
Thus $\partial A_\pi$ has full rank $2n-2$ exactly when $\pi$ traces a
Hamiltonian path of $H$. The frequency of full rank among the $n!$ orders
counts each Hamiltonian path in both orientations.

Let $p(H)$ be the minimum number of vertex-disjoint paths covering $V(H)$.
Single vertices are allowed, and the paths need not be induced.
Then $\max_\pi\rank(\partial A_\pi)=2(n-p(H))$.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
For an edge $v_iv_j$ with $i<j$, deleting either
endpoint removes the edge. There are $2i$ deletions before both endpoints,
$2(j-i)-1$ between them, and $2(n-1-j)$ after both.
Only the deletions between the endpoints contribute modulo $2$,
at $(2i,2j-1)$ and its transpose.
Grouping even indices before odd ones gives off-diagonal blocks $U,U^\top$
and zero diagonal blocks, where $U$ is $N_\pi$ without its zero first column
and zero last row. Hence $\rank(\partial A_\pi)=2\rank N_\pi$.
The upper triangular matrix $U$ has diagonal entries $a_{v_i v_{i+1}}$,
so $\det(\partial A_\pi)=(\det U)^2$ is their product in $\mathbb F_2$.

Expanding a nonzero minor of $N_\pi$ of order $r$ gives a choice of
$r$ edges with distinct initial vertices and distinct terminal
vertices. All edges point forward, so they give a cover by $n-r$ paths,
including singletons. Hence $\rank N_\pi\leq n-p(H)$. Conversely,
concatenate the paths of a minimum cover, listing each in traversal order.
Their $n-p(H)$ edges join consecutive vertices in this order. Taking
their initial vertices as rows and their terminal vertices as columns, in
order, gives an upper triangular submatrix with diagonal entries $1$,
so this order attains the bound.
\end{proof}

Let $J$ be the all-ones matrix. By Stanley's formula
\cite[Corollary, p.~171]{StanleyPaths}, the coefficient of $t^j$ in
$\det(I+t(J-N_\pi))$ over $\mathbb Z$ counts the directed paths on $j$ vertices
in the forward orientation, $1\leq j\leq n$. For $j=n$ only $\pi$ can be such a path, so
$\det(J-N_\pi)\bmod 2=\det(\partial A_\pi)$. Subtracting the last row of $J-N_\pi$
from each earlier row gives $\rank(J-N_\pi)=1+\rank N_\pi$ over
$\mathbb F_2$, since the first column of $N_\pi$ is zero.

For the $4\times4$ rook graph, counting full-rank mod-$2$ boundaries
recovers the number of oriented Hamiltonian paths. Summing the $0$--$1$
values of $\det(\partial A_\pi)$ as integers over all vertex orders gives
\[
 \#\{\pi:\rank_{\mathbb F_2}(\partial A_\pi)=30\}
 =\sum_\pi\det(\partial A_\pi)
 =\sum_\pi\prod_{i=0}^{14}a_{v_i v_{i+1}}=22{,}394{,}880.
\]
This agrees with the fourth term of OEIS A096970 \cite{OEISRook},
which counts each path and its reverse separately. The program
\program{experiments/interleaved_graph_paths.py}{interleaved_graph_paths.py}
evaluates the sum by grouping paths by endpoint and set of unused vertices.

\section{Kernel families in a product of Grassmannians}
\label{app:kernel-families}
Fix a field $K$, a degree $n$ and excesses $\mathbf c$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:generated}. Let $W$ be the affine $K$-space of degree-$n$
tensors, so that $W(F)=X^{\mathbf c}_n(F)$ for every field extension $F/K$.
Write $C_F$ and $\mathcal D_F$ for the scalar extensions of $C=N(K[\Delta^n])$
and $\mathcal D=N(X^{\mathbf c}(K))$. By representability and Dold--Kan,
$T\mapsto g_T$ identifies $W(F)$ with the space of chain maps $C_F\to\mathcal D_F$.

Let $r_p$ be the rank of $g_{T,p}$ at the generic point of $W$,
and put $\ell_p=\dim C_p-r_p$. The locus $U$ where these ranks are attained
simultaneously is dense and open. On $U$ the kernels define a morphism to
the product of Grassmannians of $\ell_p$-planes in $C_p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-map}
 \Psi:U\longrightarrow G=\prod_{p=0}^n\operatorname{Gr}(\ell_p,C_p),
 \qquad T\longmapsto (\ker g_{T,p})_p.
\end{equation}
The image of $\Psi$ in the underlying space of $G$ is constructible by
Chevalley's theorem \cite[Tag 054K]{Stacks}. Its reduced closure $Y$ is integral since $U$ is irreducible.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kernel-rationality}
Over any field $K$, the reduced closure of the kernel family $\Psi(U)$ is
birational to an affine space over $K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $W$ and $F=K(W)$. By
\eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting}, $\im g_\eta$ is isomorphic to $Q_F$ for a
complex $Q$ over $K$ with the same disk and sphere multiplicities.
By the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:kernel-realizability}, cycle and boundary
dimensions determine whether one complex embeds in another. Scalar extension
preserves these dimensions, so $Q_F\hookrightarrow\mathcal D_F$ gives an embedding
$Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal D$ over $K$. Applied to the duals with degrees reversed,
the same criterion gives $C\twoheadrightarrow Q$ from $C_F\twoheadrightarrow Q_F$.
The composite $C\to Q\to\mathcal D$ is $g_{T_0}$ for some $T_0\in U(K)$.

In $W\times Y$ let $\mathcal E$ be defined by the incidence equations
$g_{T,p}(L_p)=0$ on the tautological subbundles $L_p$.
Over $U$ the inclusion $L_p\subseteq\ker g_{T,p}$ is an equality of subbundles
of the same rank, so $\mathcal E\cap(U\times Y)$ is the graph of $\Psi$.
At $y_0=\Psi(T_0)$ the solutions are the chain maps $Q\to\mathcal D$,
since $C/\ker g_{T_0}\cong Q$. At $\Psi(\eta)$ the solutions over $F$ are the
chain maps $Q_F\to\mathcal D_F$. The solution spaces have the same dimension, so
the incidence equations have their generic rank $m$ at $y_0$.

In local bases at $y_0$ choose a nonzero minor of order $m$ and let $Y^\circ$
be the open set where it is invertible. Since $Y$ is integral, all larger
minors vanish there. Projection $\pi$ onto the $d=\dim W-m$ coordinates
outside the minor's columns identifies $\mathcal E|_{Y^\circ}$ with
$Y^\circ\times\mathbb A_K^d$ by solving the linear equations in $T$.
Intersect the open graph with $\pi(T)=\pi(T_0)$. This identifies an open
neighborhood of $T_0$ in the affine $m$-plane $\pi^{-1}(\pi(T_0))$
with an open neighborhood of $y_0$ in $Y$. In particular,
$\dim Y=m=\dim W-\dim_K\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ch}}(Q,\mathcal D)$.
\end{proof}

Over $\mathbb C$, $Y$ is isomorphic to a Schubert variety.
The subcomplexes $L\subseteq C$ with $\dim L_p=\ell_p$ form a quiver Grassmannian.
Cerulli Irelli, Feigin and Reineke embed this Grassmannian in a partial flag variety
\cite[Example~1.3, Proposition~2.1 and Theorem~2.2]{SchubertQuiver}.
In this embedding $C_p=V_p/S_p$, where a common Borel subgroup $B$ preserves
both flags $V_\bullet$ and $S_\bullet$. The inclusions $V_p\subseteq V_{p-1}$
induce the differentials, so each $b\in B$ induces a chain automorphism
$u_b$ of $C$. Precomposition by $u_b^{-1}$ preserves the ranks of
$g_T$ and sends its kernel to $u_b(\ker g_T)$. Hence $Y$ is $B$-stable.
The $B$-orbits of the flag variety are its finitely many Schubert cells.
Since $Y$ is closed and irreducible, it is the closure of one such cell.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:three-by-three}
Let $n=2$ and $\mathbf c=(0,0)$, so $T$ is a $3\times3$ matrix. On $U$
the kernel dimensions in degrees $0,1,2$ are $2,0,0$. Identify a plane
$\ker(a,b,c)$ with $[a:b:c]$ in the dual projective plane $G$. For every
field extension $F/K$, the image $\Psi(U(F))=G(F)\setminus\{[1:1:1]\}$
is one orbit under chain automorphisms of $C_F$.
For each $T\in U(F)$, $H_1(\langle T\rangle)\cong F$ and
$H_j(\langle T\rangle)=0$ for $j\ne1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Over $F$, write $T=(t_{ij})$ and $(a,b,c)=(t_{00},t_{11},t_{22})$.
In degree zero $g_{T,0}$ is the row $(a,b,c):F^3\to F$.
In degree one, normalization subtracts $t_{ii}$ from every entry of
the restriction to $i,j$, for $i<j$. In the basis $(E_{01},E_{10},E_{11})$
of $N_1(X)$ the normalized restrictions to $01,02,12$ are the columns of
\[
 B_T=\begin{pmatrix}
 t_{01}-a&t_{02}-a&t_{12}-b\\
 t_{10}-a&t_{20}-a&t_{21}-b\\
 b-a&c-a&c-b
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For fixed $a,b,c$ the first two rows are arbitrary. They can complete the
last row to a basis exactly when $a,b,c$ are not all equal. If $\det B_T\ne0$,
then $g_{T,0}$ has rank one and $g_{T,1}$ is injective. The top simplex
spans $C_2$ and has nonzero boundary, so $g_{T,2}$ is also injective.
Thus the maximal ranks are $(1,3,1)$ and $U=\{T:\det B_T\ne0\}$.
The kernel tuple is $(\ker(a,b,c),0,0)$. The row construction works over
every $F$, so it gives the asserted image and $Y=G$. The fiber over each such
plane is the open set $\det B_T\ne0$ in the seven-dimensional space of matrices
whose diagonals lie on the line spanned by $(a,b,c)$.

The augmentation $(1,1,1)$ restricts to a nonzero linear form on
$\ker(a,b,c)$, with one-dimensional kernel. The homology follows from
\eqref{eq:generated-homology}. Each quotient has chain dimensions $(1,3,1)$
and differential ranks $(0,1,1)$, so they are all isomorphic by \eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting}.
Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift} gives the orbit assertion.
\end{proof}

Over $\mathbb F_q$ there are $q^2+q$ such planes. For each one, $q-1$
diagonals represent its normal. Given the nonzero last row of $B_T$, there
are $q^3-q$ choices for the first row outside its span, then $q^3-q^2$
choices for the second outside the span of the other two. Hence each plane
occurs for $(q-1)(q^3-q)(q^3-q^2)$ matrices.
For $q=2$, \program{experiments/three_by_three_kernels.py}{three_by_three_kernels.py}
computes the kernel of each of the $512$ matrices and finds $24$ above each
of six planes, $144$ in all. Over any $K$, the all-ones matrix lies outside $U$. Its kernel is
$(\ker(1,1,1),C_1,C_2)$, and it generates the constant simplicial module with homology
$K$ in degree zero alone.
