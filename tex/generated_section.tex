\section{Generated submodules}
\label{sec:generated}
Let $K$ be a field, fix $n\geq0$ and nonnegative tail lengths
$\mathbf c=(c_1,\ldots,c_k)$, and put $X=X(\mathbf c;K)$.
Let $P=K[\Delta^n]$ be the free simplicial $K$-module on the standard
$n$-simplex, so $P_p$ has basis the nondecreasing maps $[p]\to[n]$.
For $T\in X_n$, its representing property gives
\[
 f_T:P_\bullet\longrightarrow X_\bullet,
 \qquad f_T(\alpha)=X(\alpha)(T)
 \quad\bigl(\alpha:[p]\to[n]\bigr).
\]
Its image is the simplicial submodule $\langle T\rangle$ generated by $T$,
and $P/\ker f_T\cong\langle T\rangle$. Put
\[
 C=N(P),\qquad g_T=N(f_T),\qquad L(T)=\ker g_T.
\]
Normalization is exact, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generated-normalization}
 N(\langle T\rangle)\cong C/L(T).
\end{equation}
Here $C_p$ has dimension $\binom{n+1}{p+1}$ for $0\leq p\leq n$
and is zero otherwise. Hence the finitely many spaces $L_p(T)$ determine
the entire simplicial kernel by Dold--Kan. Equality of these kernels is equivalent to an isomorphism of the
generated modules sending one distinguished simplex to the other.
For instance, $E_{01}$ and $E_{10}$ have the same kernel in
shape $(2,2)$ and generate distinct embedded submodules.

Homology is computed from normalized complexes. Since $H_0(C)=K$ and
$H_j(C)=0$ for $j>0$, the short exact sequence
$0\to L(T)\to C\to C/L(T)\to0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generated-homology}
 \begin{gathered}
 H_j(\langle T\rangle)\cong H_{j-1}(L(T))\qquad(j\geq2),\\
 0\longrightarrow H_1(\langle T\rangle)\longrightarrow H_0(L(T))
 \xrightarrow{\epsilon}K\longrightarrow H_0(\langle T\rangle)
 \longrightarrow0,
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is induced by the augmentation sending every vertex of
$\Delta^n$ to $1$.

\subsection{Quotients and automorphisms}

We work in the category $\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)$ of nonnegative
chain complexes of $K$-vector spaces and chain maps. A complex is finite
if the direct sum of its chain groups is finite-dimensional. For a complex $E$,
write
\[
 Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(E)=\ker(\partial_p:E_p\to E_{p-1}),\qquad
 B_p(E)=\im(\partial_{p+1}:E_{p+1}\to E_p),
 \qquad E_{-1}=0.
\]
Thus $Z_0^{\mathrm{ord}}(E)=E_0$. For a $K$-vector space $V$ and
$p\geq0$, let $S^p(V)$ be the complex with $V$ in degree $p$ and zero elsewhere. Choosing complements of boundaries
inside cycles, and of cycles inside each chain group, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-chain-splitting}
 E\cong\bigoplus_{p\geq0}S^p(H_p(E))
       \oplus\bigoplus_{p\geq1}D^p(B_{p-1}(E))
\end{equation}
for every finite complex $E$. Indeed, the differential maps the chosen
complement of $Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(E)$ isomorphically onto $B_{p-1}(E)$.
These choices also show that homology dimensions and differential ranks
determine the chain isomorphism class and admit cancellation under direct sums.

For the normalized simplex complex, the dimensions of its chain groups
and its homology give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex-projective}
 C\cong S^0(K)\oplus\bigoplus_{p=1}^n
                   D^p\bigl(K^{\binom np}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Both $S^0(V)$ and $D^p(V)$, for $p\geq1$, are projective objects:
a chain map from either is determined by an arbitrary linear map from
$V$ into the target's degree-zero or degree-$p$ group, respectively.
Evaluation in either degree and $\operatorname{Hom}_K(V,-)$ are exact.
Consequently $C$ is projective.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quotient-lift}
Fix a field $K$ and $n\geq0$, and let $C=N(K[\Delta^n])$.
For subcomplexes $L,M\subseteq C$, every specified chain isomorphism
$\varphi:C/L\to C/M$ lifts to a chain automorphism $u:C\to C$
making the following square commute:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 C \arrow[r,"u"] \arrow[d,"\pi_L"'] & C \arrow[d,"\pi_M"]\\
 C/L \arrow[r,"\varphi"'] & C/M .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Here $\pi_L$ and $\pi_M$ are the quotient maps. In particular,
$C/L\cong C/M$ if and only if $u(L)=M$ for some
$u\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)}(C)$.
The orbits of this automorphism group on subcomplexes of $C$ classify
the chain isomorphism classes of quotients of $C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Projectivity supplies chain maps $f,g:C\to C$ with
$\pi_Mf=\varphi\pi_L$ and $\pi_Lg=\varphi^{-1}\pi_M$.
Put $h=gf$, so $\pi_Lh=\pi_L$. Choose $m$ large enough that the
images and kernels of $h^m$ stabilize in every degree. The Fitting
decomposition is a direct sum of subcomplexes
\[
 C=U\oplus V,\qquad U=\im h^m,\quad V=\ker h^m,
\]
with $h_U=h|_U$ invertible. Since $\pi_Lh^m=\pi_L$, we have $V\subseteq L$.
Let $e:C\to U$ be the projection and define
$r=h_U^{-1}eg:C\to U$. Then $rf|_U=1_U$, so
\[
 C=f(U)\oplus V',\qquad V'=\ker r,\qquad f|_U:U\xrightarrow{\sim}f(U).
\]
Write $\lambda=\pi_L|_U:U\to C/L$. Since $V\subseteq L$ and
$\pi_Lh=\pi_L$, we have $\lambda e=\pi_L$ and
$\lambda h_U^{-1}=\lambda$. Hence $\lambda r=\pi_Lg$ and
$\pi_M=\varphi\pi_Lg=\varphi\lambda r$, so $V'\subseteq M$.

Cancellation in \eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting} gives a chain
isomorphism $a:V\to V'$. Define $u$ by $u|_U=f|_U$ and $u|_V=a$.
The two direct sums show that $u$ is invertible. Its quotient map
agrees with $\varphi\pi_L$ on $U$ and vanishes on $V$, proving the
specified lifting identity. Taking kernels gives $u(L)=M$.
Conversely, such an automorphism induces an isomorphism of quotients.
\end{proof}

For $a\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Ch}_{\geq0}(K)}(C)$,
let $\widetilde a:P\to P$ be its simplicial automorphism under Dold--Kan.
The tensor
\[
 T'=f_{T,n}\bigl(\widetilde a_n(\operatorname{id}_{[n]})\bigr)\in X_n
\]
satisfies $f_{T'}=f_T\widetilde a$ by the representing property of $P$.
Hence $L(T')=a^{-1}L(T)$ and
$\langle T'\rangle=\langle T\rangle$. Thus the set
$\{L(T):T\in X_n\}$ is invariant under the automorphism group.
Equation~\eqref{eq:generated-normalization}, Dold--Kan and
Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift} identify its orbits with the abstract
simplicial isomorphism classes of the generated modules.
There are only finitely many such orbits: the chain dimensions and
differential ranks of $C/L$ are bounded integers.

\subsection{Realizable kernels}

Put $\mathcal D=N(X(\mathbf c;K))$. A subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ is
\emph{realizable in $X$} if $L=L(T)$ for some $T\in X_n$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kernel-realizability}
Let $K$ be a field, $n\geq0$, $C=N(K[\Delta^n])$, and
$X=X(\mathbf c;K)$ for arbitrary nonnegative tail lengths $\mathbf c$.
For a subcomplex $L\subseteq C$ and $Q=C/L$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is realizable in $X$.
\item There is a chain monomorphism $Q\hookrightarrow\mathcal D=N(X)$.
\item For every $p\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realizability-inequalities}
 \dim_K Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(Q)\leq
          \dim_K Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal D),\qquad
 \dim_K B_p(Q)\leq\dim_K B_p(\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A realizing map $g_T:C\to\mathcal D$ induces the required monomorphism
$C/L(T)\to\mathcal D$. Any chain monomorphism restricts to injections
on ordinary cycles and on boundaries, proving the inequalities.

Conversely, assume \eqref{eq:realizability-inequalities}. Basis extension
gives, in each degree, an injection
\[
 j_p:Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(Q)\longrightarrow
                 Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal D),
 \qquad j_p(B_p(Q))\subseteq B_p(\mathcal D).
\]
Choose $Q_p=Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(Q)\oplus W_p$. For $p>0$,
$\partial_Q:W_p\to B_{p-1}(Q)$ is an isomorphism. Lift
$j_{p-1}\partial_Q|_{W_p}$ through
$\partial_{\mathcal D}:\mathcal D_p\to B_{p-1}(\mathcal D)$ to a
linear map $\ell_p:W_p\to\mathcal D_p$. Such a lift exists because
its image lies in the indicated boundary space. Set
$i_p(z+w)=j_p(z)+\ell_p(w)$; in degree zero use $i_0=j_0$, since $W_0=0$.
These maps form a chain map. For $p>0$, if $i_p(z+w)=0$, applying
the differential gives $j_{p-1}\partial_Qw=0$, hence $w=0$ and then $z=0$.
The degree-zero map is injective by construction. Thus
$i:Q\to\mathcal D$ is a monomorphism.

The composite $C\to Q\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal D$ corresponds under
Dold--Kan to a simplicial map $P=K[\Delta^n]\to X$. The representing
property of $P$ identifies it with $f_T$ for the image $T$ of the
identity $n$-simplex. Its normalized kernel is $L$, so $L=L(T)$.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:disks} identifies $B_p(\mathcal D)$ with the
coordinate module $Z_p$ of Section~\ref{sec:normalized} for every $p\geq0$.
For $p>0$, $Z_p^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal D)=Z_p$, whereas
\[
 Z_0^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal D)=B\oplus Z_0,\qquad
 \dim_K Z_0^{\mathrm{ord}}(\mathcal D)=\prod_a(c_a+1),\qquad
 \dim_K B_0(\mathcal D)=\prod_a(c_a+1)-\prod_a c_a.
\]
In particular, positive tails contribute ordinary degree-zero cycles
through the summand $B$. The remaining dimensions in
\eqref{eq:realizability-inequalities} are given by Corollary~\ref{cor:ranks}.

For example, when $\mathbf c=(1,1)$, the ordinary cycle dimensions
of $\mathcal D$ are $(4,2,0)$ and its boundary dimensions are $(3,2,0)$.
The complex $Q=D^1(K^4)$ is a quotient of $N(K[\Delta^4])$ by
\eqref{eq:simplex-projective}. It satisfies the cycle inequalities,
but $\dim B_0(Q)=4>3=\dim B_0(\mathcal D)$, so it does not embed.

\subsection{Matrix and sparse coordinate examples}

\begin{example}\label{ex:parameter-jump}
For $t\in K$, take empty tails and
\[
 T(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&1&1\\1&1&t\end{pmatrix}\in X_2,
 \qquad Y_t=\langle T(t)\rangle.
\]
In $N_1(X)$, use coordinates $(E_{01},E_{10},E_{11})$.
The normalized restrictions to vertex pairs $01,02,12$ are
\[
 v_{01}=(1,0,1),\qquad v_{02}=(0,1,t),\qquad
 v_{12}=(0,0,t-1).
\]
Their determinant is $t-1$. The normalized image of the top simplex
is $E_{12}-E_{21}$, whose differential is $E_{01}-E_{10}$.
The differential out of $N_1(X)$ reads the $E_{11}$ coordinate.
Thus the differential ranks of the generated complex are $(0,1,1)$,
indexed by source degree, and
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &(\dim N_0(Y_t),\dim N_1(Y_t),\dim N_2(Y_t))&(\dim H_0(Y_t),\dim H_1(Y_t),\dim H_2(Y_t))\\ \hline
 t\ne1&(1,3,1)&(0,1,0)\\
 t=1&(1,2,1)&(0,0,0).
\end{array}
\]
For $t\ne1$, the cycle
$E_{01}=v_{01}-(t-1)^{-1}v_{12}$ represents the nonzero homology class.
At $t=1$, the cycle space in degree one is the line spanned by
$v_{01}-v_{02}=E_{01}-E_{10}$, which is already a boundary.

In the vertex basis of $C_0=K^3$, the degree-zero kernel is
\[
 L_0(T(t))=\ker(0,1,t)
   =\operatorname{span}_K\{(1,0,0),(0,-t,1)\}.
\]
These planes vary with $t$. For $t\ne1$, the remaining normalized
kernels are $L_1=L_2=0$, and all the quotient complexes are isomorphic.
Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift} therefore places these distinct
kernels in one automorphism orbit. At $t=1$, $L_1$ is spanned by
the normalized edge $[12]$ and $L_2=0$. For example, $T(4)$ has
first Betti number one over $\mathbb Q$ and zero over $\mathbb F_3$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:sparse-higher-cycle}
Fix $q\geq1$, use $k=q+1$ axes and any nonnegative tail lengths,
and take $T_q=E_{(0,1,\ldots,q)}\in X_q(\mathbf c;K)$.
Every face of $T_q$ is zero, since its index tuple contains every
active label. Hence $T_q$ is normalized. All lower-degree generators
of $\langle T_q\rangle$ vanish. In higher degrees, every nondecreasing
map to $[q]$ is noninjective, so its image is a degeneracy of a
lower-degree generator. Consequently
\[
 N(\langle T_q\rangle)=S^q(K),\qquad
 H_q(\langle T_q\rangle)\cong K,\qquad
 H_p(\langle T_q\rangle)=0\quad(p\ne q).
\]
For $q=2$ and empty tails this uses one nonzero entry in a
$3\times3\times3$ tensor. For $q=3$ it uses one entry in shape
$(4,4,4,4)$ and gives one-dimensional third homology.
In the full module,
\[
 \partial_{q+1}E_{(1,2,\ldots,q+1)}=T_q.
\]
Thus the same cycle is an ambient boundary. The number of axes is
minimal: \eqref{eq:cycles} gives $Z_q(X)=0$ for $k\leq q$, so
no simplicial submodule of such an $X$ has nonzero $q$th homology.
\end{example}

The repository script \path{experiments/kernel_sequences.py} computes
the kernel, quotient and homology data in these examples.

\subsection{Graph chains in shifted matrix slices}

Let a finite graph have $v$ vertices and $e$ oriented edges, each
numbered separately starting at $1$. Write $u_j\to w_j$ for edge $j$.
For $r\geq\max(v,e)$ use $X((r,r,r);A)$. Write a tensor as a
family of matrices, with its third coordinate specifying the slice.
The following generators are supported in fixed tail slices:
\[
 a_t=E_{(0,t,t)}\in X_0,\qquad
 T_j=E_{(0,u_j+1,u_j+1)}+E_{(1,w_j+1,w_j+1)}
              +E_{(0,1,j+1)}\in X_1.
\]
Here $1\leq t\leq v$ and $1\leq j\leq e$. Deleting index $1$
gives $d_1T_j=a_{u_j}$, and deleting index $0$ gives $d_0T_j=a_{w_j}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:graph}
The simplicial submodule $Y_G$ generated by the $a_t$ and $T_j$
has normalized complex $A^e\to A^v$ in degrees $1,0$, with
differential sending edge $j$ to its target minus its source.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The vertex tensors are distinct coordinate units. The normalized
edges $b_j=T_j-s_0a_{u_j}$ are linearly independent because the
coordinate $(0,1,j+1)$ occurs with coefficient $1$ in $b_j$ and
vanishes on the other normalized edges. Also
$d_0b_j=a_{w_j}-a_{u_j}$. The other degree-one generators are
degeneracies of vertices, and every generator in higher degrees
is a degeneracy. The normalization projection kills these terms.
\end{proof}

The boundaries of the edges of a spanning forest form a basis
of the kernel of the vertex-sum morphism on each component. Subtracting
forest paths from the remaining edges gives a basis of cycles.
Thus $H_0(Y_G)\cong A^b$ and $H_1(Y_G)\cong A^{e-v+b}$, where $b$
is the number of components. The Petersen graph has ten vertices,
fifteen edges, and one component, so its first homology is $A^6$.
The script \path{rank_shifted_homology.py} computes its incidence
rank from the normalized tensor faces over $\mathbb Q$
\cite{SparseShiftedTensors}.

The two-axis cycle module in degree one has basis $E_{(0,1)}$ and
$E_{(1,0)}$ by \eqref{eq:cycles}. Over a field, this bounds first
homology of every two-axis simplicial submodule by dimension two.

\section{Kernel families over an infinite field}
\label{app:kernel-families}
Assume now that $K$ is infinite. Retain the notation $P$, $C$, $g_T$,
and $L(T)$ from Section~\ref{sec:generated}. We parameterize the kernels
on the locus where their dimensions are minimal.

For a finite-dimensional $K$-vector space $V$ and $0\leq r\leq\dim V$,
write $\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)$ for the Grassmannian of $r$-dimensional
linear subspaces of $V$. This is a projective $K$-variety whose points
over a field extension $F/K$ are the $r$-dimensional $F$-linear subspaces
of $V\otimes_K F$. Its tautological subbundle of the trivial bundle
with fiber $V$ has fiber $L$ at the point representing $L\subseteq V$.

View $W=X_n$ as an affine space. For $0\leq p\leq n$, let $r_p$ be
the maximal rank of $g_{T,p}$ and put $\ell_p=\dim C_p-r_p$.
The locus $U\subset W$ on which all these ranks are attained is a
nonempty open subset. Constant-rank kernels define a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-map}
 \Psi:U\longrightarrow G=\prod_{p=0}^n\operatorname{Gr}(\ell_p,C_p),
 \qquad T\longmapsto(L_p(T))_p.
\end{equation}
The tuples of kernels are chain subcomplexes of $C$, since $g_T$
is a chain morphism. Chevalley's theorem \cite[Tag 054K]{Stacks}
makes the image $\mathcal M=\Psi(U)$ constructible. Let $Y$ be its
reduced Zariski closure. We prove that $Y$ is rational over $K$,
that is, birational over $K$ to an affine space.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kernel-rationality}
The reduced closure $Y$ of the kernel image is rational over $K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The closure is integral because $U$ is irreducible. Each $g_{T,p}$
depends linearly on $T$. For a tuple $L=(L_p)$, the conditions
\[
 g_{T,p}(L_p)=0\quad(0\leq p\leq n)
\]
therefore define a linear subspace $V_L\subset W$.
Let $\mathcal E\subset W\times Y$ be the incidence scheme cut out by
these conditions on the tautological subbundles $L_p$ pulled back from
the factors of $G$. Over $U\times Y$,
$L_p$ and $\ker g_{T,p}$ are subbundles of equal rank, so inclusion
forces equality. Hence $\mathcal E\cap(U\times Y)$ is the graph of
$\Psi$ as a scheme, and its fiber over $L$ is $U\cap V_L$.

Let $F=K(Y)$ be the function field of $Y$, let $\eta$ be its generic
point, and write $W_F,U_F$ for base change from $K$ to $F$.
Let $V_\eta\subset W_F$ be the linear subspace defined by the incidence
conditions at $\eta$. Its intersection with $U_F$
is nonempty, since $\Psi$ is dominant onto its image closure. Put
$d=\dim_F V_\eta$. Choose $d$ coordinate functions on $W$ whose
restriction gives an isomorphism $V_\eta\to F^d$, and denote their
joint map by $\pi:W\to K^d$. Solving the defining linear equations
expresses the unique element of $V_\eta$ with $\pi(T)=c$ rationally
in the kernel coordinates and linearly in $c$. The condition that this
element belongs to $U_F$ defines a nonempty open subset of $F^d$.
Since $K$ is infinite, $K^d$ is Zariski dense in $F^d$; choose
$c\in K^d$ in that open subset.

After restricting to a nonempty open subset of
$\overline{\mathcal M}$, the subspaces $V_L$ have constant dimension,
$\pi|_{V_L}$ is invertible, and the solutions with $\pi(T)=c$
belong to $U$. Every fiber there consequently meets the affine slice
$S=\pi^{-1}(c)$ in exactly one point, varying rationally with $L$.
These solutions and $\Psi|_S$ are inverse on nonempty open subsets.
As $S\cong\mathbb A_K^{\dim W-d}$, the closure is rational.
\end{proof}

For $V=K^3$, a plane is the kernel of a nonzero linear form $(a,b,c)$,
unique up to a nonzero scalar. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Gr}(2,K^3)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathbb P_K^2,
 \qquad \ker(a,b,c)\longmapsto[a:b:c],
\]
where $[a:b:c]$ denotes the corresponding homogeneous coordinates.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:three-by-three}
For shape $(3,3)$, the generic normalized kernel has dimensions
$2,0,0$ in degrees $0,1,2$. Under the displayed Grassmannian
identification, the kernel image is
\[
 \mathcal M=\mathbb P_K^2\setminus\{[1:1:1]\}.
\]
For every $T\in U$,
\[
 H_1(\langle T\rangle)\cong K,
 \qquad H_j(\langle T\rangle)=0\quad(j\ne1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $T=(t_{ij})$ and $(a,b,c)=(t_{00},t_{11},t_{22})$.
In degree zero, $f_T$ is the row $(a,b,c):K^3\to K$.
In degree one, the columns corresponding to the three constant maps
$[1]\to[2]$ are $aJ,bJ,cJ$, where $J$ is the all-ones $2\times2$
matrix. When $(a,b,c)\ne0$, they span $KJ$. Modulo this line, the
other three columns are represented by
\[
 B_T=\begin{pmatrix}
 t_{01}-a&t_{02}-a&t_{12}-b\\
 t_{10}-a&t_{20}-a&t_{21}-b\\
 b-a&c-a&c-b
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its first two rows are arbitrary, while its last row is nonzero
exactly when $a,b,c$ are not all equal. Consequently $f_{T,1}$ can
have rank $4$ for a specified diagonal precisely when that diagonal
is nonconstant. Such a choice gives $\rank g_{T,0}=1$ and
$\rank g_{T,1}=3$, using the natural decomposition
$P_1=C_1\oplus s_0C_0$ and its counterpart for $X$.

The normalized simplex complex $C=N(K[\Delta^2])$ has dimensions
$(3,3,1)$ and injective differential $C_2\to C_1$, since its
two-dimensional simplex has nonzero boundary. Since $g_{T,1}$
is injective, the chain-map identity makes $g_{T,2}$ injective as
well. These are maximal ranks in all degrees, and
$U=\{T:\det B_T\ne0\}$. Thus on $U$ the kernel
complex is $\ker(a,b,c)$ in degree zero and zero elsewhere.
Conversely, every nonconstant projective diagonal is realized by
choosing the first two rows of the displayed matrix to complete its
last row to a basis. This construction works over every residue field.
Under the displayed identification of $\operatorname{Gr}(2,K^3)$ with
$\mathbb P_K^2$, the kernel map therefore has exactly the stated image.

The restriction of the augmentation $(1,1,1)$ to $\ker(a,b,c)$
is surjective with one-dimensional kernel, since $(a,b,c)$ is
nonconstant. Formula \eqref{eq:generated-homology} proves the homology
assertions. These quotients have the same chain dimensions and
differential ranks. By \eqref{eq:finite-chain-splitting} they are
isomorphic, and Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient-lift} places their kernels
in one automorphism orbit.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the all-ones $3\times3$ tensor generates the constant
simplicial module $K$: every simplicial operator sends it to an
all-ones tensor. Its homology is $K$ in degree zero and zero in
positive degrees. Thus generated submodules distinguish tensors by
homology even when their common ambient module is contractible.
